# FDA-regulated psychedelic trials and five-jurisdiction update (as of 11 November 2026)

Research notes for licensed medical-ethics work. Not legal advice, not a complete ClinicalTrials.gov dump, and not a claim that any investigational product is approved unless the source says so.

**Cutoff:** primary web sources retrieved 11 November 2026. Registry status can lag; NCT records and FDA dockets remain authoritative.

**How to read source tags**

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **LAW** | Statute, regulation, or final scheduling/authorization with legal effect |
| **GUIDANCE** | Agency or college guidance / practice standard (not a new drug approval) |
| **FDA ACTION** | CRL, PDAC vote, Breakthrough, hold, voucher, RFI, NDA filing (not marketing approval unless stated) |
| **PROPOSAL** | Bill, proposed rule, consultation recommendation not yet in force |
| **COMPANY** | Sponsor IR / press; verify against FDA |
| **REGISTRY** | ClinicalTrials.gov or equivalent |
| **COMMENTARY** | Journalism, academic papers, advocacy, mystery-shopper studies |

This pass is meant to deepen, not recap, earlier synthesis of Oregon Psilocybin Services, Alberta PAPT, Health Canada SAC 2026, TGA/RANZCP, CPSBC ketamine, and CANMAT.

## 1. United States — FDA, DEA scheduling, and trials

### 1.1 What is actually approved vs still Schedule I

**LAW / FDA ACTION.** As of 11 November 2026, FDA has **not** approved MDMA, psilocybin, LSD, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT (mebufotenin), ibogaine, mescaline, or 2C-B as medicines. Those remain **Schedule I** under the Controlled Substances Act unless and until FDA approval plus DEA rescheduling. Compass’s own NDA announcement (9 Nov 2026) states that a US launch of COMP360 would still require DEA rescheduling after any FDA approval ([Compass IR](https://ir.compasspathways.com/News--Events-/news/news-details/2026/Compass-Pathways-Completes-COMP360-Psilocybin-New-Drug-Application-to-U-S--FDA-for-Treatment-Resistant-Depression/default.aspx)).

**Approved comparators (not classic psychedelics):**

- **Esketamine nasal spray (Spravato, Janssen/J&J)** — FDA-approved for treatment-resistant depression (with oral antidepressant) and for depressive symptoms with acute suicidal ideation; REMS / supervised administration. Often used as the commercial TRD benchmark.
- **Racemic ketamine** — FDA-approved as an anesthetic; psychiatric use is largely **off-label**. Not Schedule I (Schedule III).

**FDA hub (updated 5 Oct 2026):** [Psychedelic Drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-drugs/psychedelic-drugs).

### 1.2 2026 federal policy stack (new since the prior chat)

These are **executive / agency actions**, not approvals:

1. **18 April 2026 — Executive Order** “Accelerating Medical Treatments for Serious Mental Illness,” directing HHS/FDA/DEA to accelerate research into psychedelic medicine, including ibogaine, and allocating at least $50 million in existing ARPA-H funds toward state programs (FDA recounts this in later press; journalism: [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/compass-usona-score-fda-national-priority-vouchers-amid-trump-administrations-psychedelic), [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/03/31/texas-ibogaine-research-clinical-trials-psychedelics/)). **PROPOSAL/EO**, not a statute changing Schedule I.
2. **24 April 2026 — FDA press:** Commissioner’s National Priority Vouchers for three programs — **psilocybin for TRD**, **psilocybin for MDD**, and **methylone for PTSD** — plus clearance of a US **Phase 1 noribogaine** IND (DemeRx NB, alcohol use disorder). FDA stresses that allowing the IND to proceed is **not** a finding of safety or efficacy ([FDA, 24 Apr 2026](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-accelerates-action-treatments-serious-mental-illness-following-executive-order)). **FDA ACTION.**
3. **July 2026 — Final guidance** *Psychedelic Drugs: Considerations for Clinical Investigations* (docket FDA-2023-D-1987), finalizing the June 2023 draft ([FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/psychedelic-drugs-considerations-clinical-investigations)). **GUIDANCE.**
4. **September 2026 — Part 15 hearing** “Considerations for Potential Future Therapeutic Use of Psychedelic Drugs” (listed on the FDA psychedelic hub). **PUBLIC ENGAGEMENT**, not a decision.
5. **5 Oct 2026 — Ibogaine RFI** (Docket FDA-2026-N-10429). Comments through **20 November 2026**. FDA is considering early-phase design elements including a starting dose **not exceeding 10 mg/kg**, sequential dose-ascending groups, cardiac/neurologic monitoring, and independent safety oversight. HHS/ARPA-H and NIDA are described as funding early ibogaine/OUD work; priority populations named: adults with OUD and adults with PTSD ([FDA, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-seeks-public-input-support-ibogaine-research)). **FDA ACTION / RFI.**
6. **FDA/VA memorandum of understanding** on data sharing for SUD and mental health (linked from the same hub). **AGENCY AGREEMENT.**

**Criticism / concern (COMMENTARY + FDA’s own language):** CDER Director Michael Davis (RFI): “serious safety concerns” with ibogaine, especially cardiac. The 2024 MDMA PDAC and CRL remain the live template for how FDA treats functional unblinding, psychotherapy as a co-intervention, abuse-potential AE capture, and durability of effect (see §1.4).

### 1.4 Lykos/MAPS MDMA-PTSD: PDAC 2024, CRL, 2025–2026 follow-on

**FDA ACTION — 4 June 2024 PDAC** (NDA 215455, midomafetamine capsules, Lykos; minutes approved 26 July 2024) ([FDA minutes PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/180463/download)):

- Efficacy vote: **Yes 2 / No 9**.
- Benefits vs risks with FDA’s proposed REMS: **Yes 1 / No 10**.
- Discussion themes: functional unblinding, expectancy/selection bias, incomplete risk characterization, need for two licensed therapists, independent training, onsite medical staff, ECG/labs/vitals, duration of impairment.

**FDA ACTION — 9 August 2024 CRL.** Lykos public statement: FDA completed review and could not approve; requested an **additional Phase 3** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lykos-therapeutics-announces-complete-response-letter-for-midomafetamine-capsules-for-ptsd-302219182.html)). Redacted CRL later posted by MAPS ([Lykos-CRL.pdf, Sep 2025 upload](https://maps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Lykos-CRL.pdf)). Core deficiencies (paraphrase of FDA letter):

1. **Safety data reliability:** sites trained **not** to report “positive/favorable” effects as AEs; FDA says abuse-potential and impairment events must be captured regardless of valence; inspections found unreported AEs.
2. **Durability:** MAPP1/MAPP2 stop at week 18; MPLONG inadequate (single visit, 6 months–2 years, self-selection, interim treatments). PTSD is chronic; three-session regimen needs labeled durability / retreatment rules.
3. **Generalizability / bias:** ~40% prior MDMA use vs ~16–20% NSDUH background in moderate/severe mental illness; high prescreen failure; expectation bias and unblinding.
4. FDA’s preferred path: **new randomized, double-blind trial** with blinded long-term follow-up, prespecified retreatment criteria, minimize prior psychedelic use, capture all abuse-related AEs, independent third-party audit of session recordings.
5. Additional (not all approvability): labs, cardiac PD/QT, **whether psychotherapy is necessary** (consider factorial / evidence-based therapy instead of MAPS method), diversity, DDI with SSRIs, hepatic/renal PK, lactation, PPND.

**2025–2026 follow-on (COMPANY / COMMENTARY, not FDA.gov confirmation of approval):**

- Staff cuts ~75%; leadership turnover after CRL (**COMMENTARY**).
- Aug 2025: recapitalization, rename to **Resilient Pharmaceuticals**.
- Aug 2026: **NDA resubmitted without new Phase 3** (company/trade). That is a **strategic bet against the CRL’s “most efficient path”** language. Whether FDA files the resubmission, issues a new CRL, or requires the extra trial is **open as of 11 Nov 2026**.

**Ethics signal:** this is the highest-resolution US regulator critique of psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy as a **drug+therapy combination product**: psychotherapy contribution, sexual-misconduct/public-hearing allegations, AE valence, and unblinding. Any US medical-ethics code that assumes “FDA will bless the MAPS dyad” is outdated.

## 2. Oregon — Psilocybin Services Act (adult-use licensed model)

### 2.1 Legal frame (do not confuse with OLCC cannabis)

**LAW.** Oregon Ballot Measure 109 / ORS chapter **475A** created a **supported adult-use** program for people **21+**, administered by **Oregon Health Authority, Oregon Psilocybin Services (OPS)** — **not** the Oregon Liquor and Cannabis Commission. It is **not** FDA medical prescribing and does **not** create a diagnosed-indication requirement. Facilitators are a **state license**, not a medical license (unless dually licensed — §2.3).

Rules: OAR chapter **333, division 333**. Tracked 24 Oct 2025 text (effective **1 Jan 2026**): [333-333_10.24.25_rules-with-redline.pdf](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/PH/PREVENTIONWELLNESS/Documents/333-333_10.24.25_rules-with-redline.pdf).

Program pages: [OPS facilitators](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ph/preventionwellness/pages/psilocybin-information-for-licensed-facilitators.aspx); [data dashboard / SB 303](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ph/preventionwellness/pages/psilocybin-data-dashboard.aspx).

**Privileges (rule, 2026 text):** service centers may sell products and provide services **6:00 AM–11:59 PM**; administration sessions must finish by midnight; **no discounts or free product**; clients may not bring in or take home product; tax collected on sales (OAR 333-333-4480).

### 2.2 Licensed capacity and 2025–2026 safety data

**LICENSE COUNTS (COMMENTARY citing public directories):** PMC public-health commentary (2026) reported, as of **3 March 2026**: **345 facilitator**, **31 service center**, **11 manufacturer**, **1 testing lab**, **724 worker** permits (vs Sep 2024: 356 / 31 / 13 / 1 / 673). Directory is opt-in and undercounts ([PMC13348737](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13348737/)). Mystery-shopper study (Jul–Sep 2025) started from 26 centers, 4 closed before calls, **22** sampled ([PMC13659005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13659005/)).

**OPS 2025 Year in Review (OHA primary, 1 Jan–31 Dec 2025)** ([PDF](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/PH/PREVENTIONWELLNESS/Documents/2025-Year-in-Review.pdf)):

- **18,868** products sold in the regulated model (includes secondary doses).
- **5,935** clients with administration sessions.
- Emergency-services contact: **13 clients (0.2%)**; 1 EMS contact per 1,451 products (0.07%).
- **0** product safety recalls in the **regulated** model (OHA contrasts 2024 CDC/OHA warnings on **unregulated** recalled products).
- Adverse reactions (OAR 333-333-1010 definitions): adverse behavioral **6 (0.1%)**, severe behavioral **7 (0.1%)**, adverse medical **13 (0.2%)**, severe medical **2 (0.03%)**.
- Behavioral-health **reasons** among those who opted into data sharing (not diagnoses; adult-use frame): anxiety 1,400; depression 1,300; PTSD 775; other trauma 584; DV trauma 329; tobacco/alcohol/SUD 291.
- Residency of data-sharing clients: **34% Oregon**, **48% other US state/territory**, **5% outside US** — destination-tourism pattern.
- Demographics (responses, not unique-client census): 76% European/White clients; income skewed toward higher brackets; 51% women.

SHIP 2025–29 includes a strategy to “destigmatize psilocybin as a culturally responsive option for healing and wellness” (**policy plan**, not a medical indication).

All-time safety slide used by OHA (1 Jan 2023–29 Jun 2026): **45,047** products sold, **32** EMS reports, **0** regulated recalls ([SHIP slides](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/PH/ABOUT/Documents/ship/OPS-SHIP-Colalborative-Slides.pdf)).

**Interpretation caution:** low EMS rates are **program-reported**. They do not equal FDA pharmacovigilance, do not capture delayed psychological harm, and do not make the model “medical.”

### 2.4 Criticisms and incidents

- **Mystery shopper (Jul–Sep 2025, 22 centers):** websites/calls to people presenting with depression often failed to give consistent eligibility, medical-risk, or “this is not treatment” information ([PMC13659005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13659005/)). **COMMENTARY / empirical.**
- **Public-health commentary (2026):** unclear how adverse-event reports change policy; federal illegality chills dual-license uptake; prices $15 microdose–$3,500/session; equity vs tourism ([PMC13348737](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13348737/)).
- **Safety incidents:** OPS publishes EMS and reaction counts (above). No regulated-model product recall through 2025 Year in Review. This pass did **not** find an OHA-confirmed death **inside** licensed administration; absence of a finding is not a proof of zero harm. Unregulated product recalls remain a stated OHA/CDC concern (2024).
- **Scope:** non-directive facilitators without a health license are **not** diagnosing or treating PTSD/depression even when clients list those reasons — a core ethics collision with “medical psychedelics” framing.

## 3. Texas

### 3.1 Ibogaine — what is law

**LAW: SB 2308 (Parker/Harris), signed 11 June 2025** ([Gov. Abbott press](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-signs-ibogaine-treatment-research-law-at-texas-capitol); bill analysis [SB02308F](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/SB02308F.htm)).

- Creates a HHSC grant/consortium path for **FDA IND** ibogaine drug-development trials (OUD, depression, PTSD, veteran-relevant conditions).
- **$50 million** state matching funds; statute contemplated **dollar-for-dollar nonstate match**, Texas commercial presence, IRB, cardiac-capable settings, Breakthrough pursuit, access/reimbursement plans.
- Effective upon passage or 1 Sep 2025.

**Operational (2025–2026):**

- HHSC selected **UTHealth Houston + UTMB** to lead **IMPACT** (Ibogaine Medicine for PTSD, Addiction, and Cognitive Trauma), with a long university list (TTU, UTHSC El Paso, UT Austin, UT Health SA, UT Tyler, UTRGV, Texas A&M, UNTHSC, Baylor, JPS) ([UTHealth Houston](https://www.uth.edu/news/story/uthealth-houston-in-collaboration-with-utmb-health-awarded-50-million-by-the-state-of-texas-to-lead-ibogaine-clinical-trials)). UTHealth: addiction focus; UT Austin/Baylor: veteran TBI.
- **31 March 2026:** Lt. Gov. Patrick and Speaker Burrows announced **no qualifying drug-company match**; Texas would “fully fund” and proceed with university-led research ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/03/31/texas-ibogaine-research-clinical-trials-psychedelics/)). Spectrum (29 Apr 2026) reported leaders talking **$100 million** after the failed match ([Spectrum](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/tx/austin/news/2026/04/29/ibogaine-update)).
- **Legal tension (COMMENTARY, Harris quoted in Tribune):** as written, HHSC **should not** disburse the $50 million without the private match; a “legislative or legal fix” may be required. Drug development to NDA will likely cost **more than $100 million**. This is **not** FDA approval and **not** legal ibogaine treatment outside trials.

Ibogaine remains **Schedule I** federally. Texas did **not** reschedule it for clinical use outside FDA pathways.

### 3.2 Psilocybin / MDMA bills — proposals that died

**NOT LAW.** 89th Regular Session:

- **HB 4014** (Bucy et al.): HHSC study of MDMA, psilocybin, ketamine for PTSD/depression with Baylor and Dell Medical School; report path; expires 1 Sep 2027. **Passed House 8 May 2025; referred Senate HHS 19 May 2025; died in committee** ([Texas Legislature history](https://capitol.texas.gov/BillLookup/History.aspx?Bill=HB4014&LegSess=89R); engrossed text [HB04014E](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB04014E.htm)).
- **HB 4813:** also **passed House, died in Senate HHS** (referred 13 May 2025).

Psilocybin possession remains a **felony** under Texas controlled-substance law. No statewide adult-use or medical-psilocybin framework.

No Texas Attorney General opinion found in this pass that **authorizes** psilocybin or ibogaine practice. University trials, if they proceed, rest on **FDA IND + Schedule I researcher registration**, not AG blessing.

### 3.3 Ketamine clinics — proposed medical-board rules (not yet law)

Ketamine-assisted / psychotropic ketamine therapy is practiced in Texas clinics under **off-label** use of an approved anesthetic. That is **existing medical practice**, not a psychedelic legalization.

**PROPOSAL (not adopted as of sources dated May 2026):** Texas Medical Board proposed new **22 TAC Chapter 173, Subchapter B, §§173.6–173.18** (Parenteral Ketamine Therapy / PKT) ([TMB proposal PDF](https://www.tmb.texas.gov/file/proposed-rule-1736-17318-may-2026); reporting [Texas Tribune 28 Apr 2026](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/04/28/texas-ketamine-regulations-medical-board/)):

- Mandatory **clinic registration** (hospice exception).
- Administration limited to physicians and midlevels under specified training; delegating physician cap (**≤6 delegates or ≤3 clinics**, whichever less).
- On-site monitoring, ASA moderate-sedation equipment, PMP checks, pregnancy verification, informed consent, time-out.
- **Ban on home-use parenteral PKT** and administration outside registered/exempt settings.
- If physician not on site, staff cannot treat more than **two patients at a time** (Tribune).
- Two-year registration; audits akin to pain-clinic rules.

**Status: proposed rule.** Confirm Texas Register adoption before treating as binding.

## 4. Canada

### 4.1 Federal criminal vs medical vs religious

**LAW.** Most classic psychedelics are **CDSA Schedule III** (psilocybin, DMT, LSD, mescaline); **MDMA and ketamine are Schedule I** (CDSA numbering ≠ US). No marketed DIN for psilocybin or MDMA.

**Three lawful medical/research doors:**

1. **Clinical trials** (Food and Drugs Act / FDR + CDSA dealer/exemption).
2. **Special Access Program (SAP)** — patient-specific, serious/life-threatening, conventional therapy failed/unsuitable/unavailable. **Jan 2022 FDR amendments** allowed restricted drugs (psilocybin, MDMA) via SAP.
3. **CDSA s. 56(1) exemptions** (research, or religious, case-by-case).

**GUIDANCE — Health Canada SAP notice, 27 Feb 2023** (still the operative stakeholder notice) ([canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/announcements/requests-special-access-program-psychedelic-assisted-psychotherapy.html)):

- SAP authorization is **not** a finding of safety, efficacy, or quality; not for early access promotion or to bypass trials.
- Setting must be psychologically safe; GMP dosage form expected.
- Class exemption can cover some SAP psilocybin/MDMA handling; **patients may not possess/transport**; use in presence of practitioner or delegate.
- ADR reporting (fatal 7 days; serious unexpected 15 days).

**SAC-MHD consensus (still current, 2026).** Scientific Advisory Committee on Mental Health Disorders met **3 Dec 2025, 28 Jan 2026, 9 Apr 2026** (17 hours) on MDD/TRD standards and **place of investigational/psychedelic therapies**. Delphi consensus published on Canada.ca ([consensus statements](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/corporate/about-health-canada/public-engagement/external-advisory-bodies/mental-health-disorders/consensus-statements.html)). Highest-signal statements:

- Comprehensive psychiatric assessment before investigational options; psychiatrist involvement **strongly recommended** for unapproved drugs in TRD.
- “Treatment resistance” requires **documented optimized** pharm + psychotherapy (SAP submissions often lacked dose/duration, augmentation, measurement-based care).
- Psychiatric **emergencies do not belong in outpatient psychedelic clinics**.
- SAP “serious/life-threatening” is hard to apply to chronic psychiatric risk without rigorous assessment.
- **Investigational therapies including PAT only after exhaustion of established options**, specialized settings, preferably trials; exceptional access for special circumstances.
- rTMS, ECT, IV ketamine, IN esketamine are **underused** relative to jumping to investigational psychedelics.
- **Business models / DTC marketing** of experimental meds may divert patients from established care (**regulator-adjacent ethics critique**).
- Encourage research including Open Label Individual Patient (OLIP) trials.

This **deepens** the prior chat’s SAC 2026 note: the committee is explicitly policing **SAP quality** and **clinic marketing**, not opening a national PAT program.

**CANMAT 2023** is cited by SAC as the MDD/DTD framework (measurement-based care; TRD as ≥2 failed adequate antidepressant trials with caveats). **GUIDANCE** (professional network), not law.

### 4.3 Indigenous / First Nations and religious (UDV analog)

**LAW — s. 56 religious exemptions.** CPHA (2024) and academic cites: since **2017**, Health Canada has permitted specified **Santo Daime and União do Vegetal (UDV)** activities with imported Brazilian ayahuasca. CBC (2025) reporting on a Nova Scotia Charter/religious-freedom ayahuasca prosecution: defence said Céu do Montréal’s exemption took **~16 years**; Health Canada affidavit: average processing now **~13 months**; **12 ayahuasca religious exemptions** granted ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/ayahuasca-religious-freedom-court-case-1.7541309); [HC exemptions page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/health-concerns/controlled-substances-precursor-chemicals/exemptions.html)).

**Practice / commentary:** Roots to Thrive (BC) and related Indigenous-led ketamine/PAT work; 2026 *Frontiers in Psychiatry* paper on implementing KAT in Indigenous health services notes Alberta’s uniqueness and sparse design literature ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1722573/full)). Ethics issues: data sovereignty, ceremonial vs medical frames, benefit-sharing — still **under-specified in college rules**.

## 5. Australia

### 5.1 Scheduling and access pathways (law)

**LAW — 3 Feb 2023 decision, in force 1 July 2023** ([TGA media release](https://www.tga.gov.au/news/media-releases/change-classification-psilocybin-and-mdma-enable-prescribing-authorised-psychiatrists)):

- **MDMA** for **PTSD** and **psilocybine** for **treatment-resistant depression**: Schedule **8** (Controlled Drugs) **only** when prescribed by a TGA **Authorised Prescriber** psychiatrist after HREC approval.
- **All other uses remain Schedule 9.**
- Products remain **unapproved** (no ARTG evaluation of quality/safety/efficacy). AP scheme supplies specified unapproved goods.
- TGA explicitly flagged **patient vulnerability** during PAT and the need for controls.

**LAW — quality:** TGO **112** (MDMA) and TGO **113** (psilocybine) commenced **6 January 2025** (AMAPP timeline citing TGA).

**LAW — process:** from 1 July 2024, SAS/AP applications via the online system.

**Special Access Scheme (SAS)** remains a separate unapproved-goods path (typically SAS-B for unapproved medicines) but the **headline 2023 change was AP + down-scheduling for two indications**.

State/territory **Schedule 8** poisons rules still apply (storage, administration on-site, no take-home). That is why TGA’s 2026 recommendations lean on **in-person psychiatrist presence at administration**.

### 5.2 May 2026 AP-scheme review — recommendations, not yet regulation

**PROPOSAL / TGA recommendation (published 26 May 2026)** after targeted consultation (83 invited, 29 responses) ([TGA news](https://www.tga.gov.au/news/news-articles/updates-authorised-prescriber-ap-scheme-requirements-when-accessing-mdma-and-psilocybine); [consultation outcome](https://consultations.tga.gov.au/medicines-regulation-division/targeted-consultation-ap-scheme-for-psychedelics/)). AMAPP notes these are **recommendations, not implemented as regulation** as of their tracker.

Four clusters:

1. **Psychiatrist competency:** evidence of PAT experience via **clinical trials** and/or **supervision by an experienced AP**.
2. **Therapy dyad:** at least one therapist registered with Psychology Board (**clinical psychologist endorsement**), Medical Board, Nursing and Midwifery Board (mental health experience), or Occupational Therapy Board, with PAT in scope; skills judged by AP psychiatrist under HREC oversight. **Widens** beyond “psychiatrist or clinical psychologist only,” but **Psychology Board** still privileges clinical endorsement (Medscape 2026 debate).
3. **AP oversight:** AP does screening and ongoing consent **in real time**; **physically present on-site at administration** (not necessarily the entire 8–10 hour day). Aligns with S8 “no unsupervised use.”
4. **Site:** medically supervised environment details required.

**RANZCP guidance updated May 2026** to follow TGA’s recommendations ([RANZCP](https://www.ranzcp.org/clinical-guidelines-publications/clinical-guidelines-publications-library/guidance-for-authorised-prescribers-of-mdma-and-psilocybin)). **GUIDANCE** for psychiatrists in Australia **and New Zealand** (college is binational) — NZ law is still Medsafe/Misuse of Drugs, not TGA.

### 5.3 Criticisms after rollout (2023–2026)

- **2023 clinical letter (PMC10291361):** TGA did not adequately consult experienced Australian PAP researchers; unclear training/AHPRA accreditation; indemnity vacuum ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10291361/)).
- **Mind Medicine Australia submission (6 Aug 2025) to TGA AP review:** alleges **regulatory overreach** vs HRECs; TGA contradicting HREC-approved protocols; **physical-presence** of psychiatrist all dosing day as de facto bottleneck; requirement for **clinical psychologist** excluding PACFA-registered psychotherapists; double handling TGA+HREC delaying patients ([MMA PDF](https://mindmedicineaustralia.org.au/wp-content/uploads/TGA-Review-of-AP-Scheme_MMA_Submission_FINAL_060825.pdf)). **ADVOCACY.**
- **Medscape (2026):** “who may sit in the dyad” remains contested; no National Board endorses a single PAT training credential; Psychology Board uniquely restrictive vs Medicine/Nursing/OT ([Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/clinicians-debate-who-should-deliver-psychedelic-medicine-2026a10016x9)).
- **Access:** still psychiatrist-gated, unapproved goods, expensive private settings; not PBS-listed. State poisons differences add friction even though scheduling is Commonwealth.

**AHPRA:** no standalone “psychedelic practitioner” registration. Conduct is via existing National Boards (Medical, Psychology, Nursing, OT, etc.). TGA explicitly said screening/consent concerns can be referred to Ahpra.

## 6. New Zealand

### 6.1 Law and Medsafe exceptional prescribing

**LAW.** Psilocybin **Class A**, MDMA **Class B1**, Misuse of Drugs Act 1975. **No approved medicines**; Medicines Act rules for approved medicines therefore do not apply in the usual way. Prescribing/supply/administration requires **ministerial authorisation** (Misuse of Drugs Regulations), **delegated to Medsafe**, with conditions.

**Two pathways** (Ministry of Health briefing, due 30 June 2025, proactively released) ([PDF](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2025-12/H2025068140-Briefing-Advice-on-psychedelic-assisted-therapy-with-psilocybin-and-MDMA-in-a-clinical-setting.pdf)):

1. **Clinical trials** (authorised use).
2. **Named-practitioner authorisation** outside research.

Officials: **do not recommend law change**; evidence still emerging; FDA declined MDMA; Australia’s TGA summary of limited evidence is cited; better to let practitioners apply and Medsafe condition authorisations. “Only one psychiatrist is presently allowed to use psilocybin in treatment, others can be expected to follow.” Medsafe developing application guidance.

**19 June 2025 — first non-trial prescribing approval** ([MoH news](https://www.health.govt.nz/news/new-zealand-psychiatrist-granted-approval-to-prescribe-psilocybin)):

- Christchurch psychiatrist (publicly identified in later commentary as **Dr Cameron Lacey**) authorised to prescribe, supply, and administer medicinal psilocybin **only** to patients **that psychiatrist** has assessed with **treatment-resistant depression**, under a defined protocol (assessments, misuse history).
- Record-keeping and reporting to Medsafe.
- **No other clinician** may prescribe unless separately authorised.
- Product remains **unapproved**; Medsafe has **not** evaluated safety/efficacy of a product.

This is **closer to Australia’s AP** than to Oregon adult use, but **narrower** (one doctor, one indication).

## 7. Cross-jurisdiction criticisms worth carrying into ethics work

1. **FDA’s MDMA CRL is the strictest primary-source critique of PAT as a combination product** (AE valence, durability, unblinding, psychotherapy necessity, sexual-misconduct signal in the public hearing). Compass/Usona NDAs will be read against that template even if the molecules differ.
2. **Ibogaine cardiac risk is now in FDA’s own RFI and Texas’s own bill analysis** — not only NGO commentary. State money ≠ solved tox.
3. **Oregon’s real-world safety numbers are uniquely public** (low EMS, nonzero severe reactions) but the **frame is adult-use**, with nearly half of clients from out of state and dual licensure only starting to medicalize prep/integration in 2026.
4. **Texas ibogaine is law for research funding, not treatment.** Psilocybin bills **failed**. Ketamine clinic rules are **proposed**.
5. **Health Canada SAC 2026 is a brake**, not an accelerator: SAP documentation quality, underuse of ECT/rTMS/esketamine, DTC business models.
6. **Australia’s 2023 first-in-world down-schedule produced a bottleneck fight** (who sits in the room, must the psychiatrist stay, PACFA vs Ahpra, HREC vs TGA). May 2026 recommendations try to clarify without yet being regulation.
7. **New Zealand chose exceptional named-doctor authorisation plus marae research rather than TGA-style class of APs or Oregon-style adult use.**
8. **Schedule I / Class A / S9 remain the default.** Approved psychiatric psychedelics in these jurisdictions, as of 11 Nov 2026: **none**, except ketamine/esketamine on their existing labels and off-label ketamine under college rules.
