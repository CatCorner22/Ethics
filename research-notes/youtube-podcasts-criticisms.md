# Psychedelic medicine ethics, harms, and what can go wrong: YouTube, podcasts, Cover Story Season 1

Research notes compiled November 2026. This file inventories **Cover Story: Power Trip** (New York Magazine / Psymposia), related print reporting, high-signal YouTube/livestreams, and other podcasts on ethics and harms—not hype. Claims are tagged **alleged**, **admitted/acknowledged**, **video-documented**, or **regulator-confirmed** where possible. Speakers and sources are named.

Primary hub for Season 1: [The Cut Cover Story tag](https://www.thecut.com/tags/cover-story/). Companion reporting: [psymposia.com/powertrip](https://www.psymposia.com/powertrip-2/). Apple: [Cover Story](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/cover-story/id1594675355).

## 1. Cover Story Season 1: *Power Trip* (2021–2022)

**What it is.** New York Magazine’s first investigative podcast series. Season 1 is titled **Power Trip**. Co-created, produced, and reported by **Lily Kay Ross** and **David (Dave) Nickles**. Hosted (Part I) by **iO Tillett Wright**. Executive producer/editor **Hanna Rosin**. Co-produced with **Psymposia**, a nonprofit that describes itself as a psychedelic-community watchdog. Theme music by Santigold. Press launch: [NY Mag, 10 Nov 2021](https://nymag.com/press/2021/11/new-york-launches-its-first-investigative-series-podcast.html).

**Core thesis (producers).** As psychedelic therapy barrels toward legalization/FDA approval, a culture that historically swept sexual and psychological abuse under the rug is training guides, advising governments, and running FDA-bound trials. Ross: she was abused, then discouraged from speaking; she now wants an honest conversation before the work goes mainstream.

**Provenance of the print story.** Episode 2 is built on Rachel Monroe’s Cut feature **[“Sexual Assault in the Amazon”](https://www.thecut.com/2021/11/sexual-assault-ayahuasca-tourism.html)** (originally 18 Jan 2017; republished with the podcast). Hanna Rosin’s companion video essay **[“You Won’t Feel High After Watching This Video”](https://www.thecut.com/2022/03/you-wont-feel-high-after-watching-this-video.html)** (22 Mar 2022) publishes excerpts of MAPS Phase 2 session video. Ross & Nickles’s later profile **[“The Trials of Rick Doblin”](https://www.thecut.com/article/mdma-psychotherapy-research-rick-doblin.html)** (11 May 2022) is the Season 1 print follow-up on MAPS/Lykos trial integrity. This is **not** a remake of Michael Pollan’s *The New Yorker* “The Trip Treatment” (2015); Cover Story is an adversarial follow-up to the Pollan-era renaissance narrative.

**Alleged vs. proven (season-level).**

### Ep. 1 — “I See a Sh\*tshow” — 30 Nov 2021 (~32 min)

**Transcript:** [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/2021/11/cover-story-podcast-goes-into-world-of-psychedelic-therapy.html)

**Synopsis.** Ross’s origin: mother’s death in a car accident, drug use, then induction into the **illegal underground** of psychedelic “guides.” Guides share a mission to legalize/medicalize in order to “promote the evolution of humankind,” and a **vow of silence**. Ross trains as a guide and later refuses the vow.

**Key claims / speakers.**

- Ross (paraphrase): “I see a shitshow, and I see coverups… a lot of people could get hurt.” She was told that telling her story would **“single-handedly destroy the psychedelic renaissance.”**
- At an underground ethics meeting, **Françoise Bourzat** (French-born trainer; CIIS-adjacent; trains Westerners in Mazatec-influenced mushroom work) stands up and **admits** she has “crossed those lines with people and had inappropriate sexual relationships with clients,” attributing it in part to “lineage,” then saying she did inner work. Ross’s first reaction: “mad respect.” The season later treats that confession as a **guru-style inoculation** rather than accountability.
- Power imbalance and **suggestibility** are introduced as structural: psychedelics dissolve ego boundaries; the guide is the person doing the dissolving.

**Alleged vs proven.** Bourzat’s past sexual involvement with clients is **her own admission** on the recording Ross describes. “Coverups” as a movement-wide practice is **investigative allegation**.

### Ep. 2 — “That’s an Old Story” — 7 Dec 2021 (~41 min)

**Transcript:** [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/2021/12/cover-story-podcast-thats-an-old-story.html)  
**Source article:** Monroe, [“Sexual Assault in the Amazon”](https://www.thecut.com/2021/11/sexual-assault-ayahuasca-tourism.html) (2017/2021)

**Synopsis.** Graduate-student Ross goes to the Ecuadorian Amazon (June 2012) to help a local leader tell his story. She is **drugged and raped** by the shaman (Monroe: she had been reassured he was “one of the good ones”). She escapes. When she tries to warn the psychedelic community, the message is **quiet, not now**—the “renaissance” (Burning Man + Silicon Valley money + Pollan-era media) is taking off.

**Key claims.**

- Ross (Monroe): ayahuasca tourism concentrates power with no governing body; U.S. State Department already warns students that operators cannot be vetted.
- Community response is framed as **reputation-first**: naming an Indigenous or underground healer as an abuser is treated as an attack on the medicine and on decolonization narratives.
- “That’s an old story” = the dismissal that sexual abuse in ceremony is already known, therefore not news, therefore not actionable.

**Alleged vs proven.** Assault is **survivor testimony** (Ross), published with names/details in 2017. No conviction is the center of the episode; the ethical claim is **institutional non-response**.

### Ep. 3 — “I Am the Wolf” — 14 Dec 2021 (~52 min)

**Transcript:** [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/2021/12/cover-story-podcast-i-am-the-wolf.html)

**Synopsis.** Composite-protected trainee **“Susan”** (voiced by Crystal Finn) enters CIIS-adjacent / Bourzat-orbit training. Mentor **Eyal Goren** (then an Associate MFT, later licensed Nov 2019—**podcast correction**) becomes sexually and psychologically entangled: eye-gazing at an ayahuasca circle, claiming to be “the wolf” in her dreams, lying in bed after mushrooms, wrapping legs around her, telling her to “give into erotic transference,” claiming “all his clients try to have sex with him.”

**Key claims.**

- Goren, via lawyer: allegations **false**; he never provided her therapy.
- NY Mag: ~200 text exchanges, friends, **checks** (memo “landscaping”; he texts “Next time, write ‘therapy’”), an airline ESA letter he wrote **as her licensed therapist**, and a **Françoise email**. They “found nothing to contradict her story.”
- Bourzat/Grossbard declined to comment on a phone call/email about the situation.

**Alleged vs proven.** **Alleged** misconduct with documentary corroboration assembled by journalists; **denied** by Goren. Licensing status was **misstated then corrected**. Susan later files with the **California BBS** (see Ep. 9). Outcome of that board process is not a Season 1 verdict.

### Ep. 4 — “Bad Hug” — 21 Dec 2021 (~38 min)

**Transcript:** [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/2021/12/cover-story-podcast-bad-hug.html)

**Synopsis.** Genealogy of the method: Mexican psychotherapist **Salvador Roquet**, “master of bad trips”—war/porn slideshows, earsplitting music, sensory overload. Bourzat and husband **Aharon Grossbard** ran drug-free Roquet-style **Convivencia** retreats for ~10 years; another mentor is **Pablo Sanchez**. Susan realizes boundary-crossing is not one rogue TA: it may be **baked into the training**.

**Key claims.**

- Roquet’s lineage includes coercive, overwhelming technique as “therapy.”
- Yensen (later, Buisson’s trial therapist) was a **Roquet student**—the underground method leaks into the **FDA trial** (explicit in later Cut video essay).
- “Bad hug” = sexualized / boundary-violating touch reframed as healing.

**Alleged vs proven.** Historical description of Roquet’s methods is **journalistic reconstruction**. That Bourzat/Grossbard ran Convivencia is **Bourzat’s own account**. Causal claim that abuse is “inherent” to the lineage is **interpretive**.

### Ep. 5 — “House Full of Sh\*t” — 28 Dec 2021 (~53 min)

**Transcript:** [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/2022/01/cover-story-podcast-house-full-of-shit.html)

**Synopsis.** Complaints multiply: therapists **meddling in marriages**, dating clients, “worse.” Bourzat is confronted; she and Grossbard eventually **step back** from co-founder roles. Bourzat’s defense is characterized (Cut refresher) as guru-standard **persecution narrative**. Grossbard is accused of relationship interference.

**Key claims.**

- Cult-like dynamics: charisma, inner circle, complaint = disloyalty.
- Consent rules between therapist and client are breached **repeatedly**, not as one-offs.
- Stepping down is partial “closure” that does not fix the training pipeline.

**Alleged vs proven.** Multiple **anonymous/named-in-podcast** client stories; Bourzat’s on-mic responses. Not a court finding against Bourzat/Grossbard in the episode.

**Part I closer (Cut refresher, 16 Mar 2022):** “By the end we are sure the underground needs to be purged and we need to call in the professionals.” Part II argues the **aboveground is another mess**.

### Ep. 6 — “Open Heart Surgery” — 1 Mar 2022 (~59 min)

**Transcript:** [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/2022/03/cover-story-podcast-open-heart-surgery.html)

**Synopsis.** Move from underground to **MAPS MDMA-assisted psychotherapy trials**. Three participants—**Meaghan Buisson** (public), **Mel** and **Leah** (first names / some identity protection)—were **success stories on paper** and **wrecked in life**.

**Key claims / quotes.**

- Media had sold “89 percent cure” / MDMA as a **cure for PTSD**. Participants with severe sexual-trauma PTSD “kicked the door down” to get in.
- **Mel** (paraphrase used throughout later coverage): like someone did **open-heart surgery**, repaired a bit, then **walked away with her chest still open**. She was still coded as improved.
- **Leah:** “I was one of the people who looked cured on paper… you can only capture the things you measure.”
- **Meaghan:** MDMA created **dependency** on the therapists; decrease in fear / increase in trust “even when that safety doesn’t exist.” Therapists (Yensen, unlicensed; **Dr. Donna Dryer**, psychiatrist, his spouse) use touch, pinning, “spread your legs,” bed-sharing during the **dosed session**. After the three experimental sessions she moves to their island (Cortes / where they live) for more therapy; Yensen begins a sexual relationship he calls consensual; she later reports **sexual assault** and therapy abuse.
- Dave Nickles: the way MDMA therapy is **discussed publicly does not match** these participants’ experiences; negative experiences are **not in the published papers**.

**MAPS response (concurrent):** [Participant Experiences & “Cover Story”](https://maps.org/2022/03/01/participant-experiences-cover-story/) (1 Mar 2022): sexual contact is a Code of Ethics violation; Yensen/Dryer barred (2018); site data **will not** be used to demonstrate efficacy/safety to FDA; violations reported to FDA, Health Canada, IRB; $15k CAD compassionate therapy funds (earlier 2019 statement). MAPS says Yensen/Dryer had already been removed for **record-keeping** failures; study site terminated 2016 for poor accrual.

**Alleged vs proven.** Sexual relationship after sessions: **MAPS-acknowledged**. Assault vs. “consensual”: **disputed**, lawsuit settled. In-session conduct: **on video**. “Success on paper / harm in life”: **participant testimony** vs. CAPS/PTSD metrics—this is the **measurement gap**, not a proven data fabrication.

### Ep. 7 — “Political Science” — 8 Mar 2022 (~63 min)

**Transcript:** [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/2022/03/cover-story-podcast-episode-7-political-science.html)

**Synopsis.** Buisson obtains MAPS’s ~90-page **End of Phase 2 FDA briefing**. Small sites, different rules, pooled “apples to oranges.” Reporters consult former FDA psych-drug reviewer **Dr. Erick Turner** and research ethicist **Dr. Emma Tumilty**. MAPS senior medical director **Dr. Michael Mithoefer** is interviewed.

**Key claims.**

- Doblin (quoted in later Cut profile, and thematically here): MAPS does **“political science,”** not science. He chose veterans as a more bipartisan sympathetic pool than women survivors of interpersonal violence.
- **Equipoise:** movement researchers already “know” it works; Tumilty analogizes to the Vatican testing holy water.
- Therapy is a **black box**: 2017 MAPS manual lists **13 modalities** that may “occur spontaneously”; high adherence is **not expected** because it might “stifle intuition and creativity.” Chief of therapy training, when interviewed, was **unfamiliar** with that allowance.
- Videos: MAPS gave **five-plus conflicting answers** about who watched them and when; then a **compliance review**. Provisional finding: Yensen/Dryer **did not follow protocol**.
- Negative experiences after termination are **not SAEs** by FDA definition, MAPS says; that is why long-term follow-up matters—but participants say their harm is invisible.
- Mithoefer: Phase 2 supervision was “best practices” but **“not as robust as we need it to be”**; FDA SPA (Special Protocol Assessment) for Phase 3.
- **Informed consent / withdrawal:** going off antidepressants was required; some who wanted meds back were told to **“trust the process”**; two participants (Cut profile) became suicidal / went to the ER. Hard to leave = **coercive** in Tumilty’s ethics frame.

**Alleged vs proven.** Document analysis is **journalistic + named experts**. FDA did advance Phase 3 after End of Phase 2. Whether Phase 2 “should not have passed” is **Tumilty’s opinion**, later echoed by **ICER (2024)** and **PDAC (2024)**.

### Ep. 8 — “Who Am I Fooling?” — 15 Mar 2022 (~51 min)

**Transcript:** [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/2022/03/cover-story-podcast-who-am-i-fooling-episode-8.html)

**Synopsis.** Post-Pollan *How to Change Your Mind* (2018), more civilians try psychedelics; **some die**. Two cases; question of **who warns** vs. **who profits from silence**.

**Case A — “Richard” and mushrooms (25 Oct 2018).** Takes psilocybin in a session, heart stops within an hour. Autopsy first: “probable acute **psilocin cardiotoxicity**.” An “interested physician” educates the coroner; cause of death **amended**: heart disease primary, mushrooms **contributing**. Episode’s point: true “mushroom overdose” is rare, but **cardiac risk in older people is under-studied**, and boosterism erases residual uncertainty.

**Case B — Jennee (“Jen”) Rothman (d. 31 Oct 2019, Oakland).** Therapist training toward legal PAT. Father **Joe** gets a 4 a.m. coroner call. Toxicology initially “no drugs,” **low sodium**. Texts about **“Holy Grail”** (identified as **5-APB**, an entactogen RC) introduced by partner **Jorge**. Hyponatremia / dehydration / RC risk. Family piecing it together after the fact.

**Key claims.** “Pollan effect” + money (clinics, training, startups) incentivizes **downplaying deaths**. Responsibility for warnings is orphaned between underground guides, clinicians, and media.

**Alleged vs proven.** Deaths are **coroner facts**; causal attribution is **contested and revised** (Richard) or **reconstructed from texts** (Jen/5-APB). Not MAPS-trial deaths.

### Ep. 9 — “The Complainers Group” — 22 Mar 2022 (~53 min)

**Transcript:** [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/2022/03/cover-story-ep-9-the-complainers-group.html)

**Synopsis.** Should *you* do psychedelic therapy? Answers vary. Accountability still fails. Title nods to **Sara Ahmed** on complaint.

**Key claims.**

- **Mel** reverses on medicalization: clinical setting promised “people with letters after their names”; instead, medicalization “put[s] something very powerful into the hands of power.” Her **underground** guide’s honesty (“I don’t know what the fuck just happened”) felt safer than clinical omniscience.
- **Susan** files a BBS declaration against Goren (mushroom session: head in lap, legs wrapped, sexualized touch, transference talk). Goren still denies. Another former client is also reporting.
- **Meaghan** still hasn’t watched all her own videos; police advised her not to. She wants a face on the harm so the next person does not walk into the black box blind.
- Correction: Meaghan’s MAPS agreement waived the right **to sue MAPS**—she had **not** previously sued MAPS. (Podcast correction.)
- Meaghan (ABC later): MAPS response compounded trauma. MAPS: barred investigators, reported to regulators, compassionate payment.

**Season button.** There is no simple “don’t” or “do.” There is a demand that **healing cultures stop treating complainers as the problem**.

## 4. Criticisms by category

### A. Sexual boundary violations / therapist-guide misconduct

**Strongest Cover Story claims:** Buisson session **video** (pinning, gagging, spooning, “spread your legs”) in an **FDA/Health Canada Phase 2 trial**; Yensen–Buisson sexual relationship **admitted**; MAPS **did not watch the tapes for years**; Ross’s Amazon assault + community silencing; Susan/Goren with texts; Bourzat’s **own admission** of sex with clients.

**Mechanism:** MDMA/psilocybin increase trust and suggestibility; long (6–8h) sessions; touch licensed in manuals as “nurturing”/bodywork; often **one unlicensed** co-therapist; underground has **no board**.

**Status:** Buisson in-session conduct = **documented**. Sexual relationship = **acknowledged unethical**. Criminal sexual assault = **alleged, settled**. Underground cases = **testimony + some documents**, denials exist.

### B. Trial misconduct, integrity, and “political science”

Pooling heterogeneous tiny Phase 2 sites; open-label mixed with blinded; dropout baselines counted; adherence not robust (Mithoefer); therapists and **adherence raters** as true believers; Doblin: “we don’t actually do science.” Cover Story + bioethicists’ **16-issue FDA complaint**. ICER 2024: evidence **insufficient**. PDAC 2024: no on efficacy and benefit-risk. FDA CRL.

**Not proven:** wholesale fabrication of CAPS scores. **Proven enough for regulators to refuse approval** on the record they had.

### C. Suggestibility, placebo, functional unblinding, false memories

Everyone can tell MDMA from placebo. Expectancy + “trust the process” + inner-healer language. Doblin’s own gloss: MDMA may help resist abuse **or increase suggestibility**. Cover Story / Monroe: recovered-memory style work in ayahuasca and Roquet lineages. Johnson/Huberman discuss memory vividly; critics worry **iatrogenic false memory** in trauma populations—more a **risk class** than a Cover Story smoking gun.

### D. Informed consent failures

Washout from antidepressants; difficulty withdrawing; “trust the process” when suicidal; participants not shown what therapy would actually look like (black box of 13 modalities). Buisson could not remember the session—consent to touch **during** MDMA is not meaningful in the usual sense. Pediatric plans (MAPS: ~80 children post-approval; Doblin “sooner you can treat them”) + Danforth on **assent**—Cover Story flags this as ethically premature given adult harms.

### E. Aftercare / integration gaps / “open-heart surgery”

Mel’s metaphor is the category. Integration sessions exist on paper; participants felt **abandoned** when the drug wore off and dependency remained. Academic analog: 9/30 Dutch truffle-retreat participants spontaneously reported integration challenges (mood swings, post-ecstatic blues, spiritual bypass, isolation)—usually transient, but the **clinical trial** version included sexual abuse and suicidality.

### F. Cult dynamics / guruism / complaint suppression

Bourzat’s honey voice, wolf dreams, “complainers group,” Ahmed. Adams: experiential verification. Conspirituality: VC + shaman-influencers. NY Mag refresher: “cultlike gurus” in Part I, **professional mess** in Part II.

### G. Underground vs clinical (neither automatically safe)

Part I: illegal guides, no accountability. Part II: cameras, IRBs, FDA—and still pinning and sex with a participant. Mel’s reversal: underground humility > clinical authority. MAPS argument: Yensen was a **rogue**, data excluded, ethics code exists. Cover Story argument: the **manual and culture** made the rogue possible; videos should have been watched in 2015.

### H. Medicalization vs spiritual / decolonial / anti-commercial

Neşe Devenot, Psymposia, Brian Pace: FDA path **bifurcates** supply (only GMP MAPS MDMA legal; street MDMA still Schedule I). Jules Evans / Noor / anthropological line (Noor’s *Making psychedelics into medicines*): medicalization can **narrow** use, raise price, and recode unapproved use as “abuse.” Mel: medicalization puts power in the hands of power. **Counter:** Yehuda/Freakonomics and House VA Republicans: only medicalization is politically acceptable.

2025 NYT (“How a Leftist Activist Group Helped Torpedo a Psychedelic Therapy”): Psymposia speakers at PDAC did not always disclose affiliation; Jacobs argues they helped sink Lykos. **KJZZ interview** with Jacobs. This is a **meta-criticism of the critics** (regulatory politics), not a cleaning of the trial record.

### I. Cardiac / ibogaine (and psilocybin cardiac uncertainty)

Ibogaine: hERG/QT, TdP, deaths in people **without** prior heart disease; CYP2D6 variability; forensic series (~27 deaths). Ontario criminal negligence: treatment despite borderline QT; death from ibogaine toxicity in withdrawal. Millar/Ambia: screening works. Cover Story Ep. 8: even **psilocybin** death certificates can read cardiotoxicity then get walked back—evidence base for older hearts is thin. Veteran-ibogaine boosterism in Congress rarely leads with TdP.

### J. Ketamine clinics, compounding, diversion

Not Cover Story’s center. Matthew Perry: clinic infusions → **refused dose escalation** → illicit/compounded ketamine → death (acute ketamine effect). CNN “Wild West”; Undark/OPB 2025: Oregon **strict on psilocybin, lax on ketamine** (no AE reporting for ketamine). Harms: bladder (“K-bladder”), dependence (unlike classic psychedelics), BP, respiratory depression, telehealth lozenges, multi-provider shopping. FDA has warned on compounded ketamine.

### K. Oregon psilocybin: safety vs equity

Measure 109: licensed facilitators, cameras, AE reporting—**harm-reduction architecture Cover Story would have wanted for MAPS**. Criticisms: **cost** (~$1,000–2,000+), center closures, no decrim, federal Schedule I steel-safe rules, two-to-one staffing, equity rhetoric vs. who can pay. Panic-attack ER transfers exist but appear rare relative to doses (facilitator anecdotes). Conservative local opposition is more “drug legalization” than ethics-of-touch.

### L. Cultural appropriation / Indigenous reciprocity

Monroe/Ross: ayahuasca tourism + assault. Harvard CSWR (Platero): “reciprocity” language used to **legitimize** selling Yawanawá-style ritual abroad without consent/benefit-share. Indigenous leaders at 2025 conference: non-Indigenous people wear names, clothes, run ceremonies for profit. Onset.to critique: reciprocity discourse can be **paternalist**. Celidwen et al. ethical principles for Indigenous psychedelic work are the constructive counterpart.

### M. Equity / access / race

Harvey: MAPS workplace whiteness; wellness pricing. Williams’s MAPS Phase 3 diversity site **closed** amid barriers (Noor). Insurance will follow FDA labels—if any—actuarially. Oregon fees undermine the equity subcommittee’s mandate. Disability papers: physical/sensory exclusion from trials = **structural ableism**; conversely, mandating experiential use of psychedelics in therapist training **excludes** people who cannot safely take them (SSRIs, psychosis history).

### N. Regulatory capture—and the inverse accusation

Cover Story/Psymposia: MAPS as movement org doing “political science,” Breakthrough Therapy PR, SPA as shield, FDA workshopping **with** the same researchers named in a complaint. Doblin donor mix (Google, Mercer, Rockefellers) as strange-bedfellows capture.

Inverse (NYT 2025, some veterans’ groups): **Psymposia capture of PDAC public comment**, left anti-commercial ideology blocking a PTSD treatment. Both can be partly true: **weak trial ethics** and **factional public-comment politics**.

### O. Feminist critiques

Cover Story is itself a feminist investigation (Ross’s PhD on responses to sexual violence). Claims: trauma trials recruit **women survivors** while marketing **male veterans**; touch protocols + MDMA = predictable sexual abuse; complaint is punished as harming “the medicine.” Dismantling Patriarchy talk; Bourzat as female guru who still reproduces the structure.

### P. Disability critiques

(1) Exclusion: psychosis, personality-disorder, physical-disability contraindications keep the most marginalized out while claiming “breakthrough for trauma.” Devenot on medicalization’s exclusionary endgame. (2) Frontiers 2022: plan for physical/sensory inclusion or reinforce ableism. (3) npj Mental Health Research 2024: **do not require** personal psychedelic use for clinicians. (4) Spiritual emergency: mystical experience pathologized in the West, unsupported after the clinic.

### Q. Right-wing / conservative critiques

Less a single YouTube canon, more **three strands**:

1. **Medical-only, anti-counterculture:** Miller-Meeks, some GOP VA hawks—PAT for veterans in clinic, **not** legalization, not Pollan-Burning-Man. Ibogaine for opioids as MAGA-adjacent (Texas, RFK-era interest) **despite** cardiac deaths.  
2. **Anti-drug / Christian:** psychedelics as occult, false mysticism, gateway (older culture-war material; lower scientific signal).  
3. **Anti-woke / NYT 2025:** Lykos was killed by **left activists**, not by science—used to dismiss Cover Story as motivated. Conservatives who want veteran MDMA thus **downplay** Buisson.

High-signal conservative ethics content is mostly (1) and the **ketamine-chaos / Perry** crime stories, not deep MAPS-manual analysis.

### R. False memories / spiritual emergency / integration-as-commodity

Roquet overload; ayahuasca “downloads”; MAPS non-directive + therapist intuition. Post-retreat spiritual emergency literature (Journal of Psychedelic Studies). Integration apps/checklists as **commodification** (2024 critical review): the more the surrounding culture cannot hold mystical content, the more “integration” is sold back as a service—and the more aftercare gaps hurt people who cannot pay.

## 5. Later FDA/PDAC fallout (post-Season 1, still Cover Story’s children)

- **2022:** Health Canada review; FDA “serious concerns” letter; videos public.  
- **2023:** House VA pro-PAT hearing (parallel political universe).  
- **Mar–May 2024:** ICER insufficient evidence.  
- **4 Jun 2024:** PDAC livestream, 9–2 / 10–1 nos. Public comment includes Cover Story themes (abuse, unblinding, therapy cult).  
- **Aug 2024:** FDA CRL to Lykos (not approval).  
- **2025:** NYT Jacobs piece on Psymposia’s role; ongoing fight over whether the **science failed** or the **activists won**.

Cover Story did **not** “prove” MDMA cannot treat PTSD. It **did** put on the record that the leading Phase 2/3 program mixed **movement fervor, weak video oversight, sexual abuse, measurement that missed devastation, and a psychotherapy the FDA does not know how to regulate.** PDAC and ICER then refused to treat that program as approval-grade evidence.
