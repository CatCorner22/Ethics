## Executive synthesis

Licensed psychedelic care should keep the ethical **floor of medicine and law** (honest indication, capacitous consent, non-exploitation, safety, confidentiality) and add duties ordinary prescribing does not cover: hours-long reduced autonomy, suggestibility, touch, and a clear choice between **monitored dosing (Track A)** and **psychotherapy (Track B)**.

**Track A — medically monitored dosing (no talk therapy).** Indication, screening, consent, witnessed dosing, physiologic and behavioral monitoring, rescue, discharge, structured check-in. Acute-session speech limited to safety, orientation, and pre-agreed comfort. Do not run unlicensed trauma processing. Offer independent integration resources. Medical follow-up is monitoring, not PAT.

**Track B — psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy.** All of A plus a named licensed psychotherapeutic method (preparation, dosing-session sitting within a therapy contract, integration). Prefer two trained persons in the room. If prescriber and therapist are the same person, disclose dual role and add oversight. Training should not be solely by a product sponsor.

**Capacity rule.** Before dosing, bind optional preferences. During acute effect: safety plus the pre-dose plan; in-session “stop” always wins. After recovery: new consent for later sessions—never treat intoxicated speech as sexual or financial consent.

**Hard legal floor as of 11 November 2026:** FDA has **not** approved MDMA, psilocybin, LSD, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT (mebufotenin), ibogaine, mescaline, or 2C-B as medicines. Those remain **Schedule I** in the US unless and until FDA approval plus DEA rescheduling. Across Oregon, Texas, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, approved psychiatric **classic** psychedelics: **none**. Ketamine/esketamine sit on existing anesthetic/REMS labels plus off-label ketamine under college rules.

**There is currently no national or international binding ethics code specific to medical psychedelic use.** Existing instruments are profession-general (AMA, APA psychiatry/psychology, RANZCP, college codes), trial-sponsor (MAPS Code v4), membership (ASKP3), underground/defense (ICEERS/ADF), or adult-use facilitator rules (Oregon OAR 333-333, Colorado 4 CCR 755-1). Psychiatry APA 2022 and AMA H-100.943 remain investigational-only. WHO, INCB, WMA Helsinki 2024, UNESCO, CIOMS, VA/DoD CPGs, and FDA guidance do **not** supply a PAT ethics code.

**Ethics signal from the MDMA record:** FDA’s 2024 PDAC and the **8 August 2024** complete response letter (CRL; Lykos announced **9 August 2024**) are the highest-resolution US critique of PAT as a **drug + therapy combination product**. A code that assumes “FDA will bless the MAPS dyad” is outdated. Cover Story Season 1 put sexual misconduct, unwatched session video, aftercare abandonment, and “success on paper / harm in life” on the public record; PDAC and ICER then refused to treat that program as approval-grade evidence. Cover Story did **not** prove MDMA cannot treat PTSD.

### The 24 concepts (full list)

These sit **on top of** existing license ethics. They are for physicians, NPs, PAs, psychiatrists, and, where relevant, nurses, pharmacists, and psychotherapists on the team.

1. **Indication honesty and least-misleading frame.** Documented clinical indication; name evidence grade; call off-label/investigational/SAP what it is. Do not present mystical or personality change as a medical benefit unless it is part of the disclosed, evidence-based indication. Breakthrough, vouchers, executive orders, and NDA **filings** are not efficacy.
2. **Capacitous consent before ingestion, freeze on optional new permissions.** After ingestion the patient may tighten restrictions; clinician may not loosen them except true emergency/safety care. Do not expand touch consent after dosing.
3. **Three-tier touch plus body-map double consent.** Safety touch always; supportive touch only with written pre-dose agreement and stop signals; sexual touch never. In-session refusal always wins. Pre-dose refusal of supportive touch is not overridden because the intoxicated patient “asks.”
4. **Anti-capture: beliefs, money, testimonials, wills.** No soliciting business, donations, investments, media, or romance in the suggestible window. No influence over wills or POAs. Disclose philosophical setting in advance.
5. **Medical screening, setting, and rescue as non-delegable duties.** Do not strip other psychotropics without a documented plan. Cardiac and autonomic monitoring scaled to the drug.
6. **Track A: do not smuggle in unlicensed psychotherapy.**
7. **Track B: licensed therapy frame, two-person default, independent (not sponsor-only) training, extra dual-role caution.** Consented video is a legitimate abuse-deterrent **if someone actually watches**.
8. **Fiduciary conflict disclosure against conventional care and against volume.**
9. **Confidentiality with NOSC-specific limits**, including groups, command, recordings, and compelled records (WCB, insurers, employers, courts).
10. **Equity, cultural non-imposition, and Indigenous eight Rs** (not branding). FPIC and benefit-sharing in writing if commercially dependent.
11. **Competence without mandatory personal use.** Change-of-scope when the college requires it. Facilitators must not consume product while serving.
12. **Impaired-patient protective action, limited to harm** (ABA 1.14 analog).
13. **Continuity, non-abandonment, and delayed harm.** Plan post-trial/post-course care before the first session.
14. **Accountability architecture** that can leave the building (college/board, FDA/Health Canada—not only internal or PI-routed).
15. **Clinical equipoise and no ballot-box medicine.** Health Canada SAC: established options first (including underused ECT/rTMS/esketamine).
16. **Enhanced consent for unknowable and identity-level effects.** Name the Jacobs–Villiger tension; preparation extends consent **and** primes set.
17. **Most-responsible clinician present in the dosing-day chain** — not a virtual prescriber plus unlicensed sitters.
18. **No take-home psychedelic; pharmacist as independent stop.** Spravato-like supervised analog where it fits.
19. **Treatment not enhancement; harm-reduction talk without illegal assistance.**
20. **Strictest-license sexual boundary and special-population gates.** Default to the strictest clock in the room (often lifetime / five years +, not Colorado’s two-year facilitator clock).
21. **Name support versus psychotherapy and bill only what occurred.**
22. **Drug-class risk floor (ibogaine ≠ ketamine ≠ classic).** Match staff and equipment to the molecule.
23. **Do not blur medical, sacramental, and adult-use frames.**
24. **Aftercare, fees, ads, and virtual limits as ethics.** Virtual prep/integration does not replace in-person dosing accountability.

### Operational tests after Pass 4

1. Can you name the **legal frame** (FDA investigational, SAP, TGA AP, Medsafe named doctor, Oregon adult-use, Texas off-label ketamine, sacramental s.56/RFRA) in one sentence without mixing them?
2. For this **molecule**, what is the risk floor (ibogaine ECG vs ketamine sedation/diversion vs classic suggestibility)?
3. Are you doing **support or psychotherapy**, and would a video audit show only what you billed?
4. Who watches recordings, and where does a complaint go **outside** the building?

## FDA / US trials landscape

### What is actually approved vs still Schedule I

**LAW / FDA ACTION.** As of 11 November 2026, FDA has **not** approved MDMA, psilocybin, LSD, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, ibogaine, mescaline, or 2C-B as medicines. Compass’s COMP360 NDA announcement (9 Nov 2026) itself states that a US launch would still require **DEA rescheduling** after any FDA approval.

**Approved comparators (not classic psychedelics):**

- **Esketamine nasal spray (Spravato, Janssen/J&J)** — FDA-approved for treatment-resistant depression (with oral antidepressant) and for depressive symptoms with acute suicidal ideation; REMS / supervised administration. sNDA 211243/S-016 signed **17 January 2025** expanded the indication to **monotherapy** of Spravato for TRD.
- **Racemic ketamine** — FDA-approved as an anesthetic; psychiatric use is largely **off-label**. Schedule III, not Schedule I.

FDA hub (updated 5 Oct 2026): [Psychedelic Drugs](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-drugs/psychedelic-drugs).

### 2026 federal policy stack (executive / agency, not approvals)

1. **18 April 2026 — Executive Order** “Accelerating Medical Treatments for Serious Mental Illness,” directing HHS/FDA/DEA to accelerate research including ibogaine, and allocating at least $50 million in existing ARPA-H funds toward state programs. **PROPOSAL/EO**, not a statute changing Schedule I.
2. **24 April 2026 — FDA press:** Commissioner’s National Priority Vouchers for **psilocybin for TRD**, **psilocybin for MDD**, and **methylone for PTSD**, plus clearance of a US **Phase 1 noribogaine** IND (DemeRx NB, alcohol use disorder). Allowing an IND to proceed is **not** a finding of safety or efficacy.
3. **July 2026 — Final guidance** *Psychedelic Drugs: Considerations for Clinical Investigations* (docket FDA-2023-D-1987), finalizing the June 2023 draft. **GUIDANCE.**
4. **September 2026 — Part 15 hearing** “Considerations for Potential Future Therapeutic Use of Psychedelic Drugs.” Public engagement, not a decision.
5. **5 Oct 2026 — Ibogaine RFI** (Docket FDA-2026-N-10429). Comments through **20 November 2026** (**OPEN / pending**). FDA is considering early-phase design elements including a starting dose **not exceeding 10 mg/kg**, sequential dose-ascending groups, cardiac/neurologic monitoring, and independent safety oversight. CDER language: “serious safety concerns,” especially cardiac.
6. **FDA/VA memorandum of understanding** on data sharing for SUD and mental health.

#### MDMA (midomafetamine) — Lykos / Resilient

**FDA ACTION — 4 June 2024 PDAC** (NDA 215455): efficacy **Yes 2 / No 9**; benefits vs risks with proposed REMS **Yes 1 / No 10**. Themes: functional unblinding, expectancy/selection bias, incomplete risk characterization, need for two licensed therapists, independent training, onsite medical staff, ECG/labs/vitals, duration of impairment.

**FDA ACTION — 8 August 2024 CRL** (OpenFDA PDF for NDA 215455; Lykos press **9 August 2024**). Requested an **additional Phase 3**. Core deficiencies (paraphrase of FDA letter posted by MAPS):

1. **Safety data reliability:** sites trained **not** to report “positive/favorable” effects as AEs; inspections found unreported AEs; abuse-potential and impairment events must be captured regardless of valence.
2. **Durability:** MAPP1/MAPP2 stop at week 18; MPLONG inadequate. PTSD is chronic; three-session regimen needs labeled durability / retreatment rules.
3. **Generalizability / bias:** ~40% prior MDMA use vs ~16–20% NSDUH background; high prescreen failure; expectation bias and unblinding.
4. FDA’s preferred path: **new randomized, double-blind trial** with blinded long-term follow-up, prespecified retreatment criteria, minimize prior psychedelic use, capture all abuse-related AEs, independent third-party audit of session recordings.
5. Additional: labs, cardiac PD/QT, **whether psychotherapy is necessary** (factorial / evidence-based therapy instead of MAPS method), diversity, DDI with SSRIs, hepatic/renal PK, lactation, PPND.

**2025–2026 follow-on (COMPANY / COMMENTARY, not FDA.gov confirmation):** staff cuts; Aug 2025 recapitalization and rename to **Resilient Pharmaceuticals**; Aug 2026 **NDA resubmitted without a new Phase 3**. FDA acceptance / PDUFA **not confirmed on FDA.gov as of 11 Nov 2026** (**OPEN**). That resubmission is a strategic bet against the CRL’s “most efficient path.”

## Jurisdictions: Oregon, Texas, Canada, Australia, New Zealand

### Oregon — LAW: supported adult-use, not medical prescribing

**LAW.** Ballot Measure 109 / ORS chapter **475A**. Administered by **Oregon Health Authority, Oregon Psilocybin Services (OPS)** — **not** OLCC. Age **21+**. No diagnosed-indication requirement. Facilitators are a **state license**, not a medical license unless dually licensed. Rules: OAR chapter **333, division 333** (24 Oct 2025 text, effective **1 Jan 2026**). Privileges: service centers 6:00 AM–11:59 PM; administration sessions finish by midnight; **no discounts or free product**; clients may not bring in or take home product; tax on sales (OAR 333-333-4480). Default **non-directive** facilitation. Facilitator must not consume product while serving.

**Capacity and 2025–2026 safety data (OHA 2025 Year in Review):** 18,868 products sold; 5,935 clients with administration sessions; EMS **13 clients (0.2%)**; **0** regulated-model product recalls. Adverse behavioral 6 (0.1%); severe behavioral 7 (0.1%); adverse medical 13 (0.2%); severe medical 2 (0.03%). Behavioral-health *reasons* among data-sharing clients (not diagnoses): anxiety 1,400; depression 1,300; PTSD 775. Residency: **34% Oregon**, **48% other US**, **5% outside US** — destination tourism. Demographics skewed White and higher-income. All-time slide 1 Jan 2023–29 Jun 2026: 45,047 products, 32 EMS reports, 0 regulated recalls. **Caution:** low EMS ≠ FDA pharmacovigilance and does not capture delayed psychological harm.

Unregulated product recalls remain a stated OHA/CDC concern (2024). Non-directive facilitators without a health license are **not** diagnosing PTSD/depression even when clients list those reasons.

Colorado (adjacent, not a core five-jurisdiction refresh but used in codes/best practices): 4 CCR 755-1 (2024) — consent each session; touch contract; sexual/romantic ban **two years** post-termination; dual-licensees follow the **stricter** code. New Mexico Medical Psilocybin Act (SB 219 signed April 2025, effective 20 June 2025): NMDOH goal of first patients by end of December 2026; statute allows implementation through 31 December 2027.

### Texas

**LAW: SB 2308 (Parker/Harris), signed 11 June 2025.** HHSC grant/consortium path for **FDA IND** ibogaine drug-development trials (OUD, depression, PTSD, veteran-relevant). **$50 million** state matching funds; statute contemplated dollar-for-dollar nonstate match, Texas commercial presence, IRB, cardiac-capable settings, Breakthrough pursuit, access/reimbursement plans.

**Operational:** HHSC selected **UTHealth Houston + UTMB** to lead **IMPACT**. **31 March 2026:** no qualifying drug-company match; leaders talked “fully fund.” Spectrum (29 Apr 2026) reported talk of **$100 million**. **Legal tension (COMMENTARY):** as written, HHSC **should not** disburse the $50 million without the private match; a legislative or legal fix may be required (**OPEN**). This is **not** FDA approval and **not** legal ibogaine treatment outside trials. Ibogaine remains **Schedule I** federally.

**NOT LAW.** HB 4014 and HB 4813 (MDMA/psilocybin/ketamine studies) passed House, **died in Senate HHS**. Psilocybin possession remains a **felony**. No statewide adult-use or medical-psilocybin framework. No AG opinion found authorizing psilocybin or ibogaine practice.

### Canada

**LAW.** Most classic psychedelics are **CDSA Schedule III** (psilocybin, DMT item 7, LSD, mescaline); **MDMA is Schedule I**; **ketamine is Schedule I, item 14(1)** (listed under phencyclidine — not a “Part 1” label). **5-MeO-DMT is not named** on CDSA Schedules I–III; Health Canada treats it as an NPS whose sale is under the Food and Drugs Act. Ibogaine is not CDSA-scheduled but is on the Prescription Drug List (19 May 2017) and is **not authorized** for use. No marketed DIN for psilocybin or MDMA.

**Three lawful medical/research doors:** (1) clinical trials; (2) **Special Access Program (SAP)** — Jan 2022 FDR amendments allowed restricted drugs (psilocybin, MDMA) via SAP; (3) CDSA **s.56(1)** exemptions (research or religious, case-by-case).

**GUIDANCE — Health Canada SAP notice, 27 Feb 2023** (still operative): SAP is **not** a finding of safety, efficacy, or quality; psychologically safe setting; GMP dosage form expected; patients may **not** possess/transport; use in presence of practitioner or delegate; ADR reporting (fatal 7 days; serious unexpected 15 days).

**SAC-MHD 2025–26 (consensus on Canada.ca): a brake, not an accelerator.** Meetings 3 Dec 2025, 28 Jan 2026, 9 Apr 2026. Highest-signal statements: comprehensive psychiatric assessment; psychiatrist involvement **strongly recommended** for unapproved drugs in TRD; “treatment resistance” requires documented optimized pharm + psychotherapy; psychiatric **emergencies do not belong in outpatient psychedelic clinics**; investigational therapies including PAT **only after exhaustion of established options**; rTMS, ECT, IV ketamine, IN esketamine are **underused**; **DTC marketing** of experimental meds may divert patients from established care; encourage research including OLIP trials. **CANMAT 2023:** serotonergic psychedelics for MDD experimental; trials first; SAP rare. **GUIDANCE**, not law.

### Australia

**LAW — 3 Feb 2023 decision, in force 1 July 2023:** **MDMA** for **PTSD** and **psilocybine** for **TRD** are Schedule **8** **only** when prescribed by a TGA **Authorised Prescriber** psychiatrist after HREC approval. All other uses remain Schedule **9**. Products remain **unapproved** (no ARTG evaluation). TGO **112** (MDMA) and TGO **113** (psilocybine) from **6 January 2025**. From 1 July 2024, SAS/AP applications via the online system. State/territory S8 poisons rules still apply (storage, on-site administration, **no take-home**). TGA flagged **patient vulnerability** during PAT.

**PROPOSAL / TGA recommendations 26 May 2026 (not yet regulation — OPEN):** after targeted consultation (83 invited, 29 responses). Four clusters: (1) psychiatrist competency via trials and/or experienced-AP supervision; (2) therapy dyad widened (Psychology Board **clinical psychologist endorsement**, Medical Board, NMBA mental-health experience, OT Board) under AP/HREC — still contested (PACFA exclusion; Medscape 2026); (3) AP physically **present on-site at administration** (not necessarily the entire 8–10 hour day); (4) medically supervised site details. **RANZCP** guidance updated May 2026 — **GUIDANCE** for psychiatrists in Australia **and New Zealand**; **does not create NZ prescribing rights**.

**Criticisms:** 2023 clinical letter (PMC10291361) on consultation/indemnity/training vacuum; Mind Medicine Australia (6 Aug 2025) alleges overreach vs HRECs, presence bottleneck, PACFA exclusion, double handling; AHPRA has no standalone “psychedelic practitioner” class. Access remains psychiatrist-gated, unapproved, expensive, not PBS-listed.

### New Zealand

**LAW.** Psilocybin **Class A**, MDMA **Class B1**, Misuse of Drugs Act 1975. No approved medicines. Two pathways: clinical trials, or **named-practitioner authorisation** delegated to Medsafe. Officials (briefing due 30 June 2025, proactively released): **do not recommend law change**; evidence still emerging; FDA declined MDMA; Australia’s limited-evidence summary cited.

**19 June 2025 — first non-trial prescribing approval:** one Christchurch psychiatrist (later identified in commentary as **Dr Cameron Lacey**) authorised to prescribe, supply, and administer medicinal psilocybin **only** to patients **that psychiatrist** has assessed with **TRD**, under a defined protocol, with reporting to Medsafe. **No other clinician** may prescribe unless separately authorised. Product remains **unapproved**. Narrower than Australia’s AP class.

**Research:** **EMMAC** (Auckland & Otago) MDMA-assisted therapy for mood/anxiety in end-stage cancer; Inner Healing Intelligence language and te reo **ihi**. **Marae-based rongoā PAT** for methamphetamine use disorder — HRC Māori Health Clinical Training Fellowship 2025, 36 months, $265,000; endemic psilocybin fungi as rongoā; Rangiwaho Marae, Te Tairāwhiti. NZ is the jurisdiction in this set where **Indigenous protocol is being built into a funded trial design**, not only named as a principle. That does not make rongoā psilocybin a licensed medicine. No separate NZ “psilocybin facilitator” license.

### Cross-jurisdiction criticisms for ethics work

1. FDA’s MDMA CRL is the strictest primary-source critique of PAT as a combination product.
2. Ibogaine cardiac risk is now in FDA’s own RFI and Texas’s own bill analysis. State money ≠ solved tox.
3. Oregon’s real-world safety numbers are uniquely public but the frame is **adult-use**, with nearly half of data-sharing clients from out of state.
4. Texas ibogaine is law for **research funding**, not treatment. Psilocybin bills **failed**. Ketamine clinic rules are **proposed**.
5. Health Canada SAC 2026 is a **brake**, not an accelerator.
6. Australia’s 2023 first-in-world down-schedule produced a bottleneck fight (who sits in the room, must the psychiatrist stay, PACFA vs Ahpra, HREC vs TGA). May 2026 recommendations try to clarify without yet being regulation.
7. New Zealand chose exceptional named-doctor authorisation plus marae research rather than TGA-style AP class or Oregon-style adult use.
8. Schedule I / Class A / S9 remain the default. Approved psychiatric classic psychedelics in these jurisdictions as of 11 Nov 2026: **none**.

## Bioethics and philosophy journals (by theme)

Inventory: **62** distinct papers/books/consensus statements given full or table-level treatment in the appendix, plus Helsinki 2024, FDA PDAC, ICER, CPHA, MAPS-adjacent reports. Core venues: JME, AJOB / AJOB Neuroscience, Neuroethics, CQHE, KIEJ, JAMA Network Open, JAMA Psychiatry, JBI, PPP / Perspectives in Biology and Medicine, Philosophy Compass, Frontiers. Paywalled full texts were not always available.

### A1. Transformative experience, consent, and non-ordinary states

**L. A. Paul (2014), *Transformative Experience*.** Epistemic and personal transformation breaks standard expected-utility consent. Critics (Callard on aspiration; later PAT literature) argue people routinely consent to parenthood, education, and surgery under similar ignorance.

**Edward Jacobs (2023), *Frontiers in Psychology*.** Applies Paul to PAT. Enhanced disclosure can block unauthorized bodily trespass but cannot guarantee *values-aligned* choice if values themselves change. Preparation primes “set.”

**Smith & Sisti (2021), *JME*.** Consent is possible with *enhanced* disclosure: ego dissolution, metaphysical or personality change, mental-health risks, therapeutic touch. Research-trial protections will not automatically travel with commercialisation. Print: *J Med Ethics* 2021;47(12):807–814; first published online **27 May 2020**.

**Daniel Villiger (2024), *Neuroethics*.** Patients typically know they want to leave a bad status quo. Phenomenal ignorance of “what it will be like” does not block value-aligned choice if the *function* of treatment is understood. Aspiration (Callard) across a course of sessions.

**Kious, Peterson et al. (2024), *Perspectives in Biology and Medicine*.** Limited evidence that psychedelic experiences are transformative in Paul’s strict sense; “consent is impossible” would disable large parts of medicine.

**Peterson, Largent, Lynch, Karlawish, Sisti (2023), *AJOB Neuroscience* — “Journeying to Ixtlan.”** AD/ADRD research-ethics agenda: fluctuating capacity; ego dissolution colliding with a pathology of self; caregiver influence; exploitation of desperation.

**Lee et al. (2024).** Advance directives for PAT; *dynamic* consent across preparation, dosing, and integration; trustful relationship *before* the first drug session.

**2026 conceptual analysis, *Journal of Psychedelic Studies*.** Agency most compromised **acutely**. Avoid “psychedelic ethical exceptionalism.”

**Villiger (2025), *Philosophy Compass*.** Field map: consent camps; safety and therapist influence; unblinding; personal-use requirements; comforting delusion; medicalization vs spiritual use. Criticises Pollan-effect hype and historical amnesia.

*Practice:* keep concepts 2/16; name the Jacobs–Villiger tension in the consent form; freeze optional permissions after ingestion.

### A2. Authenticity, identity change, and belief

**Nielson (2022):** authenticity is *intersubjective* — easy for a therapist to colonise. **Devenot et al. (KIEJ 2022):** “set and setting” steers identity; non-directiveness is often a curated mystical protocol. **Timmermann et al. (2021) *Scientific Reports*:** measurable shifts away from physicalism toward panpsychism lasting ≥6 months. **Letheby (2021) *Philosophy of Psychedelics*:** naturalistic entheogenics answers the comforting-delusion objection; unmitigated epistemic cost remains ethically serious. Critics say this recentres Western naturalism. **Yaden, Earp, Griffiths (2022) vs Rasmussen & Olson (2022), CQHE:** whether the trip is *necessary* is unsettled; do not engineer meaning out by default; do not lock in a high-vulnerability model prematurely. **Timmermann, Watts, Dupuis (2022):** *apprenticeship* for validating insights without guru capture or “it’s just the drug” epistemic injustice (**Zeller 2024**). **Thomas & Silva (2025):** chemically scaffolded emotion can still be meaningful.

### A3. Therapist power, suggestibility, REBUS, and exploitation

**Villiger & Trachsel (JME 2023):** if REBUS is right, suggestibility is the **mechanism of benefit and of harm**. “Trust, let go, be open” without disclosing dependence is unethical. Two therapists; limits on interpretation during the session.

**McNamee, Devenot, Buisson (*JAMA Psychiatry* 2023):** AEs poorly defined and under-reported; the understudied therapeutic component is the highest-risk class; stop routing harms only to the PI.

**Harrison (2025) “Wolves among sheep”; Villeneuve & Prescott (2022):** unlicensed co-therapists; MAPS manuals prohibit sex but under-specify *when* touch-consent is obtained; “never abandon” vs reported neglect.

**Villiger (2024) CQHE:** consider a trusted other in the room as monitor; precautionary line on touch.

**Barber & Dike (2023) *Psychiatric Services*:** limited evidence; unblinding; equity; ketamine gold-rush.

**Kious, Schwartz, Lewis (2022):** researcher personal use as confirmatory bias.

**Emmerich & Humphries (2024) / Banbury:** personal psychedelic use **not required** for training or licensure.

### A4. Touch, sexual boundaries, and the body

**HOPE (2024):** sexual touch never; group *divided* on therapeutic/supportive touch; honour any refusal; two-stage consent as precautionary option.

**McGuire / Banbury *JAMA Network Open* (2024):** twenty consensus points. Safety touch always; sexual never; supportive by shared decision-making; mid-session refusal honoured; conservative honoring of **pre-dose refusal** if the intoxicated patient “changes their mind”; personal experience not required for licensure.

**Neuroethics 2025/26 qualitative + 2026 practical guidelines:** body-map including atypical erogenous zones; nonverbal withdrawal; do not expand consent after dosing (**McHerron 2025**).

**FSMB (2020):** sexual behaviour between physician and patient is never diagnostic or therapeutic.

### A5. Indigenous knowledge, medicalization, epistemic injustice, spiritual bypass

**Celidwen, Redvers et al. (2023), *Lancet Regional Health – Americas*:** eight Rs in four clusters — Acknowledgement (Reverence, Respect); Knowledge (Responsibility, Relevance); IP (Regulation, Reparation); Belonging (Restoration, Reconciliation). Criticisms: token presence; enclosing patents; extractive tourism; unsustainable harvest; cartel violence; “one pill to enlightenment”; the term “psychedelics” as a Western reduction of Spirit medicines. Clinic-level: FPIC, benefit-sharing, no enclosure, ecosystem care.

**Schenberg et al.:** epistemic injustice of double-blind molecular trials over millennia of Indigenous use; Amazon dieback; ILO 169 FPIC; CBD benefit-sharing.

**HOPE §I and Banbury** “reparations and reciprocity” with recorded disagreement on co-optation vs adaptation.

**Spiritual bypass** (Welwood): using mysticism to avoid trauma work *or* using Indigenous ceremony as a wellness product.

### A6. Research ethics, Helsinki, FDA/MDMA, unblinding

**WMA Declaration of Helsinki (2024):** plain-language consent including funding, COI, incentives, harm compensation; independent consent-takers in dependent relationships; placebo only if methodologically necessary and not exposing people to additional serious irreversible harm.

**FDA PDAC ethics-inside-benefit–risk** as above. **2025 perspective (PMC11967080):** FDA says it does not regulate psychotherapy yet counted psychotherapeutic sexual-violation risk in the **drug’s** benefit–risk — paradigm clash (molecule vs drug×setting).

**Critical appraisal (PMC11713989):** suppressing AE knowledge violates non-maleficence; ICER’s darker reading; Australia’s TGA AP path vs FDA rejection.

**Aday, Muthukumaraswamy et al.:** unblinding fixes (naive-only, concealment) themselves raise Helsinki issues.

**Otterman (JBI 2022):** justice argument to fund anorexia trials. **Sisti et al. (2023):** pediatric PAT exceptional-benefit-only.

### A7. Ketamine, microdosing, enhancement, and “support vs psychotherapy”

Three-role ketamine split (mental-health professional / medical professional / patient). **KAP Code of Ethics (2024):** MAPS-style code ported to legal ketamine. Petrie-Flom on microdosing criminalization vs classist retreats. **Goodwin et al. vs PAT orthodoxy:** successful trials often used **psychological support**, not named psychotherapy. **Richard & Levine (2025)** propose observable microskills. Ethical stakes: billing, competence, Track A vs B, FDA worry that therapy inflates expectancy. Ibogaine microdosing does **not** waive cardiac screening (Brunt 2026).

### A8. Scope of medicine, access, special populations, MAID, military

**AMA Journal of Ethics (Nov 2024):** TRD vs wellness/enhancement. **CQHE 2024:** is meaning-making still *medicine*? **Rosenbaum et al. (2023) *AJOB Neuroscience*:** PAT near MAID (values-shifting drug on a death-hastening path). Military / veterans: command confidentiality, deployability, dual loyalty; Helsinki independent consent-taker. Carceral: no sentence-incentive coercion. Adolescents: exceptional benefit. Groups: confidentiality among participants. Couples: who is the client if one partner’s values change.

### A9. Exceptionalism, hype, and criticisms of the field’s ethics

**AJOB 2024** “Distinctive but not exceptional”: distinctive features do not justify a separate evidentiary *standard* (do not skip RCTs; do not declare consent impossible). **AJOB 2025** survey of exceptionalism. **Yaden/Earp/Griffiths / HOPE §X:** Pollan effect is itself an informed-consent problem. **Brittain et al. (JBI Aug 2025)** scoping review of 51 articles: clusters on standards, equity, integrity, appropriation, epistemic justice; gaps: **post-trial care**, no consensus on care models or competencies, weak translation of research ethics into ordinary prescribing. **Marks, Cohen et al.:** REMS cannot substitute for licensure ethics. **ICER** used by critics of published MDMA benefit.

### A10. Additional high-yield papers (short)

Smith & Appelbaum (2022); Brennan & Belser (2022) on touch; Johnson, Richards, Griffiths (2008) safety screens; Nayak, Griffiths et al. on personality/openness; Carhart-Harris & Friston (2019) REBUS; Stauffer et al. (2022) TGD inclusion; Schipper et al. Cochrane protocol palliative PAT; Canadian Journal of Bioethics / Monash Bioethics Review on ketamine clinics, SAP, TGA AP; Hastings Center Report still thin on PAT; JLME on FDA, state legalization, religious exemptions.

## National professional ethics codes

**Limits.** Association codes bind **members**; licensing colleges/boards bind **practice**. Dual-licensees follow the **stricter** instrument. ACA 2014 remains current pending a 2026 revision (public comment expected early 2026). CMA announced 2026 wording on racism/discrimination on top of the 2018 Code. AAMFT’s revised Code is effective 1 January 2026. NAADAC/NCC AP Code effective 1 June 2025. UK GMC is included as an **enforceable-practice template**, not because the UK is a licensed PAT jurisdiction comparable to TGA AP (**OPEN gap**).

### 18-family taxonomy

1. **Dignity, respect, non-discrimination, cultural safety** — Te Tiriti / TRC / Indigenous chapters; no imposition of practitioner values/religion. *PAT: identity and metaphysics; Celidwen; chaplain non-proselytization.*
2. **Beneficence / non-maleficence / patient welfare first** — experimental status; ICER/FDA harm; post-trial care gap.
3. **Autonomy, capacity, informed consent, freedom of consent** — unestablished-technique extra disclosure; dynamic/enhanced consent; freeze after ingestion.
4. **Confidentiality, privacy, records, compelled disclosure** — groups; recordings; WCB/subpoena; military command.
5. **Competence, scope, innovation, unestablished methods** — Track A vs B; CAP five-year rule; no mandatory personal drug use as credential.
6. **Loyalty, conflicts, dual/multiple relationships, anti-capture** — noetic capture; wills/POAs; volume incentives.
7. **Sexual boundaries** — clocks diverge: APA psychology ~2 years + burden; ACA/CRPO 5 years; AAMFT/MAPS never; Colorado facilitators 2 years. *Apply the strictest license in the room.*
8. **Touch / physical contact** — almost absent from medical/nursing except ordinary care; NASW 1.10; PAT specialty three-tier.
9. **Abandonment, continuity, interruption, termination, coverage** — integration and post-trial care.
10. **Impairment** — practitioner; analogically the patient’s NOSC (ABA 1.14). Facilitator must not dose.
11. **Honesty, advertising, fees, records as property** — hype, guru narratives, suggestible-window testimonials.
12. **Research, innovation, evidence, equipoise** — Helsinki/TCPS2/Common Rule/NHMRC/NEAC; unblinding; independent harms research.
13. **Safety, environment, collaboration, most-responsible clinician** — three-role ketamine split; two-person default; no virtual-only IV.
14. **Justice, access, public health, stewardship, environment** — who is in trials; retreat classism; ecosystem harvest.
15. **Accountability, self-regulation, complaints, mandatory reporting** — complaint path that leaves the building.
16. **Education, supervision, students** — no sexual with supervisees; sponsor-only training criticised.
17. **Technology, distance practice, social media, recording** — recording as abuse-deterrent vs marketing asset; virtual prep ≠ dosing accountability.
18. **Special populations and dependent relationships** — children, prisoners, MAID, dementia (Ixtlan), unequal relationships (NHMRC 4.4).

## Best practices by drug (from PR #3)

Copied onto this branch as [`psychedelic-best-practices-by-drug.md`](psychedelic-best-practices-by-drug.md). Pass 5 live-checked original `[verify]` flags and applied corrections. Substance is integrated here so this document stands alone.

### How to read evidence tags

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Approved** | A regulator has approved the product for the stated indication (label governs). |
| **Trial-grade** | Randomized trial data exist but no approval for this indication. |
| **Consensus / expert opinion** | Protocols from trial manuals, regulator documents, or expert panels. |
| **Anecdotal / unregulated** | Observational, case reports, or retreat experience. |

Common limits: small samples, **functional unblinding**, high expectancy, short follow-up, under-reporting of harms, trial populations that exclude many real-world patients.

### Cross-cutting floor (every drug unless a section says otherwise)

- **Screening:** psychosis spectrum, bipolar I, first-degree family history of either, active suicidality, severe personality disorder, substance use, cardiovascular and hepatic status, pregnancy/lactation, full medication review (serotonergic agents, MAOIs, lithium, QT-prolonging drugs, CYP2D6/3A4). Johnson, Richards & Griffiths 2008 remains the usual starting point.
- **Setting:** quiet, private, medically equipped room; emergency access; no sharp objects/unsafe exits; two trained people for hours-long sessions where feasible.
- **Monitoring:** continuous presence; periodic BP/HR; written escalation criteria; clinician able to treat medical emergencies reachable.
- **Preparation / integration:** at least one preparation contact and a post-session follow-up within days. Track A should say so and refer for integration.
- **Discharge:** oriented and steady; no driving or high-stakes decisions that day (longer for some drugs); accompanied transport.
- **Adverse events:** record, treat, report; college/board complaint route.
- **Diversion:** no take-home Schedule I-type psychedelics outside a sanctioned program; locked storage; pharmacist as independent checkpoint.

### 1. Psilocybin

**Status.** US Schedule I; Compass (COMP360, TRD, 2018) and Usona (MDD, 2019) Breakthrough. Oregon Measure 109 (supervised adult-use since 2023), Colorado Proposition 122 (healing centers from 2025), New Mexico Medical Psilocybin Act 2025 (SB 219; first patients targeted end-2026). State programs ≠ FDA approval. Canada: CDSA Schedule III; SAP from Jan 2022; Alberta 2023 standards.

**Evidence.** Phase 2: Carhart-Harris 2021 vs escitalopram; Davis 2021 *JAMA Psychiatry*; Goodwin 2022 *NEJM* COMP360 TRD dose-finding; Bogenschutz 2022 AUD. Phase 3: Compass IR 23 June 2025, COMP005 met week-6 MADRS primary; COMP006 and Usona uAspire. CANMAT 2023: low-level, experimental.

**Practice.** Typical trial oral doses ~10–25 mg; sessions 6–8 hours; eyeshades/music common. Screen: psychosis/bipolar I personal or first-degree; unstable CVD/uncontrolled HTN; pregnancy; lithium; active mania; severe untreated suicidality. Do not stop serotonergic antidepressants casually. Monitoring: continuous presence; BP/HR; rescue (reassurance first; benzodiazepine only per protocol). Integration 24–72 hours and again within weeks. AEs: transient anxiety, nausea, headache, elevated BP/HR; less commonly HPPD, prolonged distress, psychosis/mania; SI can occur at follow-up. Sexual-boundary violations are a documented risk class for PAT generally. Lower classical abuse liability than stimulants/opioids; still Schedule I custody.

### 2. MDMA

**Status.** US Schedule I. PDAC 4 Jun 2024; CRL **8 August 2024** (OpenFDA NDA 215455 PDF; Lykos press 9 August 2024) asking for another trial. Canada Schedule I; SAP; trials. Australia TGA AP for PTSD since 1 Jul 2023.

**Evidence.** Mitchell 2021 and 2023 *Nat Med* phase 3 large PTSD improvements vs placebo with therapy. Critics: unblinding, expectancy, therapist-effect, incomplete cardiac data, missing AE/abuse-potential capture, phase 2 sexual misconduct.

**Practice.** Trial manuals: 80–120 mg with optional supplemental half dose ~1.5–2 h later; ~8-hour sessions with two therapists; often overnight stay. Screen: uncontrolled HTN/CVD, arrhythmia/QT, hepatic impairment, pregnancy, psychosis/bipolar I, SSRI/SNRI/MAOI washout under supervision, hyponatremia risk (do not overhydrate). Monitor BP/HR/temperature; hyperthermia, hyponatremia, arrhythmia. Integration within ~24 hours; suicide risk screening; post-session “low mood” days. AEs include jaw clenching, fatigue, insomnia; documented therapist boundary violations. Recreational adulteration is a harm-reduction issue; programs need secured storage. FDA discussed REMS including external safety reporting.

### 3. Ketamine (racemic) and esketamine (Spravato)

**Status.** Ketamine: Schedule III anesthetic; psychiatric use **off-label**. Spravato: FDA Mar 2019 TRD with REMS; later MDD with acute SI; monotherapy for TRD **17 January 2025**. FDA Oct 2023 warning on compounded ketamine. DEA telehealth CS flexibilities: **fourth temporary extension through 31 December 2026** (21 CFR 1307.41). Canada: ketamine CDSA Schedule I, item 14(1); esketamine NOC **2020-05-20**. CPSBC: IV only in accredited facility. CPSO: CAM + OHPIP. KATA/OMA Psychedelic MIG 2023.

**Evidence.** Strongest rapid-antidepressant evidence in this set: Berman 2000; Zarate 2006; Murrough 2013; Popova 2019; ESCAPE-TRD Reif 2023 *NEJM*. Effects often short-lived without maintenance. Sanacora et al. 2017 *JAMA Psychiatry* consensus.

**Practice.** Spravato 56 or 84 mg IN; twice weekly ×4 weeks then weekly/biweekly; **supervised self-administration**; ≥**2 hours** observation; no driving until next day after restful sleep. IV racemic typically 0.5 mg/kg over 40 minutes. Screen per label (aneurysm, AVM, ICH history) plus uncontrolled HTN, psychosis, SUD, pregnancy, severe hepatic impairment, raised ICP, bladder symptoms. Monitor BP, HR, SpO2 (IV/IM), sedation, dissociation; CPSBC emphasises **post-dose sedation**, not vitals alone. Three-role split. AEs: dissociation, BP rise, nausea, sedation; urinary toxicity and liver enzymes with chronic use; abuse/misuse; diversion (Matthew Perry case). Spravato REMS bars take-home; compounded take-home is contested.

### 4. LSD

**Status.** US Schedule I; Canada Schedule III. Not approved. Definium DT120/MM120: P2b GAD published; three **Phase 3** trials (Voyage NCT06741228, Emerge NCT06941844, **Panorama NCT06809595**); company GAD filing targeted H1 2027. Swiss compassionate-use LSD-AT since 2014.

**Evidence.** Gasser 2014/2015 life-threatening-illness anxiety; Holze et al. Basel; Robison et al. 2025 *JAMA* (DOI 10.1001/jama.2025.13481) P2b GAD dose-ranging. Phase 3 running; no approval.

**Practice.** Microgram doses (~25–200 µg ranges); **8–12 hour** sessions plus tail. Same classic-psychedelic screens; lithium caution; physical stamina matters. Illicit-market NBOMe substitutes sold as LSD are dangerous.

### 5. Ayahuasca and DMT

**Status.** US: DMT Schedule I; ayahuasca treated as containing a controlled substance. *Gonzales v. UDV* (2006); Santo Daime *Church of the Holy Light of the Queen* Oregon federal ruling (2009). Exemptions = **religious practice**, not medical. Canada: DMT Schedule III item 7; s.56 UDV/Santo Daime since 2017. Colorado: regulated natural medicine is only psilocybin/psilocyn until **1 June 2026**; DMT **may** be added later if recommended.

**Evidence.** Palhano-Fontes 2019 *Psychol Med* small RCT in TRD; Gable 2007; synthetic DMT SPL026 Phase 2a (NCT04673383) **COMPLETED** and published *Nat Med*. Not an NDA-stage US program.

**Practice.** MAOI interaction danger (SSRIs/SNRIs, tramadol, DXM, stimulants, MDMA, some cold medicines) → serotonin syndrome or hypertensive crisis; tyramine; psychosis/bipolar I; CVD; pregnancy; pheochromocytoma. Brew potency unstandardized — medical settings need analytically verified product. Vomiting/diarrhea (“purge”) → dehydration/aspiration. Do not claim ceremonial authority; do not blur church and clinic. Deaths reported in unregulated retreats (interactions, cardiac events, trauma, sexual assault allegations).

### 6. 5-MeO-DMT (including toad-derived)

**Status.** US Schedule I. Canada: **not named** on CDSA schedules; sale under the Food and Drugs Act (NPS example). BPL-003 P2b completed + Lilly close; GH001 P2b completed, FDA hold lifted **5 Jan 2026**, P3 NCT **still unverified**. Reckweg 2021 *Front Pharmacol* (P1 HV); 2022 *J Neurochem* (review); 2023 *Front Psychiatry* (P1/2 TRD).

**Practice.** Very short, intense onset (minutes). Medical readiness for sudden loss of control, vomiting, hypertension, rare arrhythmia. Contraindicated with MAOIs. Toad (*Incilius alvarius*) sourcing: **ecological** and cultural concerns; prefer synthetic (Celidwen 2023).

### 7. Ibogaine / noribogaine

**Status.** US Schedule I. Texas SB 2308 2025 matching-fund research ($50M); Arizona **$5M** FY2026 (SB 1735 → Barrow). Canada: not CDSA-scheduled; PDL 19 May 2017; not authorized for use. Most treatment in Mexico and elsewhere, outside US/Canadian regulation. Pass 4: noribogaine US P1 IND allowed Apr 2026; FDA ibogaine RFI Oct 2026.

**Evidence.** Mostly observational; Cherian 2024 *Nat Med* magnesium–ibogaine in veterans with TBI (cardiac monitoring); Noller 2018; Brown & Alper 2018 retrospective OUD.

**Practice.** **Cardiac risk is the central issue** (hERG/QT, TdP, sudden death; Alper, Stajić & Gill 2012 *J Forensic Sci* 57(2):398–412). Exclude/manage: baseline long QT, bradycardia, structural heart disease, K/Mg abnormalities, liver impairment, QT-prolonging drugs, strong CYP2D6 inhibitors; also psychosis and seizure disorders. **Microdosing does not waive cardiac screening.** Setting: continuous ECG, defibrillation, IV access, clinician trained to treat torsades; 24–36 hours of effects plus longer elimination tail. After OUD treatment, **overdose risk rises as tolerance falls** — naloxone and continuing addiction care. Deaths have occurred in supervised settings. No take-home. Do not present as a “cure.” Veteran politics is not a cardiac waiver.

### 8. Mescaline (peyote, San Pedro, synthetic)

**Status.** US Schedule I with federal **NAC peyote** exemption (21 CFR 1307.31; AIRFA amendments 1994) — religious, not medical. Canada: Sch. III item 17 mescaline **but not peyote**; no 21 CFR 1307.31-style NAC exemption. Colorado: mescaline from **non-peyote** sources **may** be added after 1 June 2026 if recommended. Evidence: Phase 1 PK/PD only; no US Phase 3.

**Practice.** Treat as long-acting classic (10–12 h). Peyote is ecologically threatened and culturally central; non-Native clinics should prefer synthetic or cultivated alternatives (San Pedro) and avoid extracting peyote. Do not blur NAC sacramental frame with medical practice.

### 9. Other agents

**Nitrous oxide:** small TRD trials (Nagele 2015 *Biol Psychiatry*; Nagele 2021 *Sci Transl Med* 13:eabe1376); B12/neuropathy; scavenging. **Psilocybin/LSD analogs and novel short-acting agents** (deuterated psilocin, 5-MeO-DMT formulations): trial protocols only. **Cannabis:** co-use; psychosis risk and sedation. **Salvia, arketamine, psychoplastogens:** mostly preclinical/early. **MDMA analogs (MDA, 6-APB):** not recommended outside research.

## Best practices process (from PR #3)

Copied in full onto this branch as [`psychedelic-best-practices-process.md`](psychedelic-best-practices-process.md). Almost none of these practices has been tested in a randomized trial of **practice-level** outcomes (e.g. “recording reduces misconduct”). Support is consensus, analogy, and documented failures. Treat “evidence” as **strength of rationale**, not proof of effect.

| Practice | Rationale strength | Direct psychedelic evidence of effect | Main trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Informed consent in stages (Smith & Sisti *JME* 2021 print / 2020 online) | Strong (general medical ethics) | Qualitative/consensus only | Preparation can prime expectations |
| Recording of active sessions | Moderate (deterrence, review) | None on outcomes | Privacy, subpoena, chilling effects |
| Two-person dosing teams / chaperone | Moderate to strong (AMA, FSMB) | Regulator/trial standards, no outcome trials | Cost, patient comfort, collusion |
| Touch consent protocol | Moderate | Consensus (JAMA 2024; McHerron 2025) | Rigid rules vs relational care |
| Cooling-off / no-sales period | Moderate by analogy | None | Delay, access, paternalism |
| Clinical supervision/consultation | Strong in psychotherapy | Indirect | Cost, availability of qualified supervisors |
| Structured follow-up | Strong (safety) | Trial protocols | Resource use |
| Independent complaint routes | Strong (professional regulation) | Indirect | Fear of retaliation; slow processes |
| Provider wellbeing supports | Moderate (burnout literature) | Almost none | Hard to separate from marketing |

### 1. Informed consent: content and timing

Stage it: written information early; discuss in preparation; **reconfirm** on dosing day before ingestion while capacitous. Do not obtain first-time consent on dosing day under time or payment pressure. Content: indication and evidence grade; investigational/off-label/SAP status; alternatives including no treatment; COI and fees; time course; physical and psychiatric risks; possible lasting changes in values/beliefs/relationships; possibility of no benefit; suggestibility; who will be in the room; recording; touch; limits of confidentiality including compelled records. Capacity rule as in concept 2. Teach-back; copy of the document; explicit right to withdraw. Oregon: OAR 333-333-5000 / 5040 / 5050. Colorado: 4 CCR 755-1 §6.5.

**Criticisms.** Transformative-experience limit on “understanding”; preparation primes expectancy; long forms become liability shields.

### 2. Session recording

Offer AV recording of the active dosing phase with separate consent; stated retention; role-based access (not marketing); storage as a health record; patient right to pause (e.g. toileting) logged; defined review use (quality, complaint, supervision). CAP June 2025 attributes the recording recommendation to **CPSA** and requires psychologists to obtain written consent defining storage, duration, and access. Recording existed in the MDMA program, yet misconduct was exposed through other channels — recording alone is insufficient. **No RCT** of recording as a harm-reduction intervention was located (Pass 5). **Criticisms:** dignity, chilling effect, subpoena, coerced consent if treatment is conditional, false assurance if no one reviews footage.

### 3. “No more sales” and cooling-off

No soliciting packages, memberships, donations, investments, or testimonials during the acute window and immediately after. Delay between information and payment/commitment (e.g. 24–72 hours for non-urgent packages). Analogy: FTC three-day cooling-off rule; elective-surgery waiting periods; HOPE/APA psychiatry financial-capture concerns. Suggested design: pay for **evaluation** immediately; cool off before prepaying multi-session packages; prohibit upselling within a set window after a session (e.g. 7 days). **Criticisms:** access burden; paternalism analogy to contested waiting periods; clinics may rebrand “memberships.”

### 4. Chaperones and two-person teams

Two trained persons for hours-long sessions wherever feasible, at least one a licensed clinician. Offer a chaperone for intimate examination/touch (AMA Opinion **1.2.4 Use of Chaperones**, issued 2016; FSMB 2020). Do not use a family member as the “chaperone.” Chaperones need training, a stated reporting duty, and independence. Documented misconduct occurred in a **pair-based** program — team size does not replace oversight. **Criticisms:** cost; observation discomfort; bystander collusion; doubled confidentiality exposure; small-community scarcity.

### 5. Touch consent

Three tiers as in concept 3. Double consent (APA psychiatry). Do not expand after dosing (McHerron 2025). Body-map including atypical erogenous zones. Strictest cooling-off among team licenses. **Criticisms:** rigid no-touch vs comfort-as-safety; any touch in a suggestible state vs true consent; cultural differences.

### 6. Supervision, consultation, and training

Named most-responsible clinician for dosing-day decisions (CPSO ICRC; RANZCP). Independent (not solely sponsor-run) training. Personal use **not** a requirement or credential. Facilitators must not consume the substance while working (Oregon). **Criticisms:** supervisor shortage; box-checking; cost concentrating care in wealthier clinics.

### 7. Post-session follow-up and aftercare

Same-day safe discharge; **contact within 24 hours**, again within about a week, and at set intervals; structured SI/mania/psychosis/sleep/mood screening; 24/7 contact route; Track B integration or Track A **independent** integration list; plan post-trial care before session one (Brittain 2025 gap); measurement-based outcomes (SAC 2026). Spravato REMS demonstrates structured post-dose observation. **Criticisms:** unpaid follow-up; “integration coach” is an unregulated label; required integration as revenue upsell.

### 8. Complaint processes and accountability

Plain-language policy at intake; named person independent of the treating clinician; no retaliation; direct college/board and FDA/Health Canada/police routes; mandatory reporting of colleagues’ sexual misconduct; annual aggregate AE/boundary review; external (not sponsor-only) safety reporting. **Criticisms:** vulnerable patients may not complain; non-disparagement clauses; slow regulators; conflicted peer reviews.

### 9. Provider wellbeing

Caseload and session-length limits; debriefing; supervision/personal therapy; vicarious-trauma training; protection from overbooking pressure; no services while impaired (Colorado); fair pay for second-person roles. Align ANA 2025 provisions 5–6; CNA 2025; CMA self-care. Simionato & Simpson 2018 *J Clin Psychol* 74(9):1431–1456. **Criticisms:** “wellbeing” language can be used to justify mandatory personal psychedelic experience, which consensus papers reject.

### 10. Other practices

Public Track A vs Track B service description; COI disclosure; advertising limited to evidence-grade claims; tell patients which notes exist and who may compel them; independent consent-taker in dependent relationships (Helsinki 2024); outcome and AE registry participation.

**Open process questions (also listed below):** which safeguards reduce harm vs ritual; recording vs privacy/subpoena; cooling-off vs access; how to credential “integration” without a profession-wide standard.

### Societies, cases, jurisdictions (highest-signal)

- **Sexual-boundary clocks collide.** MAPS v4: **never** with participant, former participant, spouse/partner, or immediate family. Oregon facilitators: **one year** after last service (OAR 333-333-5120). Colorado facilitators: **two years**. ICEERS/ADF: **six months** after the relationship ends. APA Psychology: generally **two years** plus burden of no exploitation. ASKP3: **no numbered clock**. Dual-licensed Oregon facilitators: the **enforceable** state floor is one year; MAPS “never” binds only while MAPS-certified.
- **Oregon 2025:** 51 complaints, 60 investigations, **22 final orders** (18 civil penalties, **4 revocations**, 0 suspensions). EMS contacted **13** times / 5,935 clients (0.2%). Published orders read in this pass are mostly paper/presence/advertising, not sexual-misconduct adjudications. InnerTrek (Tom Eckert) fined **$3,000**. OPS **approves curriculum; it does not license training schools**.
- **Colorado split regulator:** DOR licenses businesses; **DORA** licenses facilitators. Official DORA table **2 Feb 2026**: **7** Natural Medicine Facilitator and **2** Clinical Facilitator applications *approved*. Journalism that “DORA issued 124 facilitator licenses” (CBS, Apr 2025) **does not match** that table — treat 124 as **unverified**. **No published facilitator disciplinary file** found.
- **Australia AP in practice:** TGA FOI unique APs 1 Jul 2023–31 Jul 2026 = **63 MDMA / 60 psilocybin**. Patients through 31 Dec 2025: **123 MDMA / 65 psilocybin**. TGA told a FOI requester **zero** AP-scheme AEs through Dec 2025 — **spontaneous reporting, not safety**. May 2026 TGA recommendations are **not** a Poisons Standard change. RANZCP called widening the therapy dyad an **“unjustifiable risk to patient safety.”**
- **Board cases that exist are mostly ketamine, not Oregon/Colorado psilocybin.** Virginia: Patrick Anthony Oliver, MD (summary suspension Feb 2024). Colorado: Mark Young, MD (Apex Ketamine) criminal + board suspension. Ontario: Tatiana Zdyb, psychologist, registration **revoked**. Yensen/Buisson: civil settlement; CPSBC Donna Dryer irrevocable resignation (1 Aug 2023); three *Psychopharmacology* Phase 2 papers retracted Aug 2024; **no psychology board** for unlicensed Yensen; Crown declined charges.
- **HB 2387 (Oregon)** imports clinical work into prep/integration while administration stays non-therapy. APA psychiatry 2026 FDA letter: if a product is FDA-approved, state non-medical credentials **must not** substitute for health licensure — a **direct collision** with adult-use facilitator models.

### International, Indigenous, veterans, Lykos research ethics (highest-signal)

- **No WHO/INCB PAT standard of care.** ECDD/CND 2024–2025 scheduled nitazenes, HHC, carisoprodol — **not** psilocybin, MDMA, or DMT. INCB encourages research and warns about wellness tourism; it does not write clinical ethics.
- **Treaties control isolated alkaloids, not most plants.** 1971 Schedule I: LSD, MDMA, psilocybin/psilocine, DMT, mescaline. Ibogaine and ketamine are **not** internationally scheduled. INCB: ayahuasca, peyote cactus, iboga shrub, and psilocybe mushrooms are **not** internationally controlled. Art. 32(4) reservations are narrow, time-of-accession, and **exclude international trade**.
- **Religious vs medical firewall.** US AIRFA + 21 CFR 1307.31 vs RFRA case law; Canada CDSA s.56 organization-specific ayahuasca exemptions (~12). Ceremony does not authorize clinics; medicalization does not automatically protect ceremony, conservation, or Indigenous IP.
- **Gabon ABS is the only working psychedelic Nagoya pilot** (Blessings of the Forest + Terragnosis/Filament, May 2023 first Nagoya-compliant iboga export). **United States is not a CBD/Nagoya Party.** Celidwen eight Rs remain COMMENTARY.
- **Veterans are the political on-ramp; ethics infrastructure has not caught up.** NDAA FY2024 §723 DoD process; VA PIVOT COMP360 (`NCT07226232`); Texas SB 2308 is **research-funding law**, not treatment authorization. **No treaty, WHO, WMA, VA/DoD CPG, or Helsinki paragraph** addresses command influence, deployability, security-clearance spillover, or disability-rating effects of PAT.
- **Lykos/FDA PDAC (4 Jun 2024)** remains the research-ethics template: functional unblinding (~90% drug / ~75% placebo guessed assignment), AE valence (positive effects not captured), session video (FDA asked for independent audit), dual therapist–investigator roles. Helsinki 2024 tightens dependent-relationship consent and data rules but **does not mention psychedelics**.

### What NO existing code, guideline, regulator, or commentator addresses

Academic papers exist for some of these; that is **not** institutional coverage. No treaty, WHO guideline, WMA paragraph, FDA ethics code, VA/DoD CPG, UNESCO recommendation, or national licensing-federation instrument was found for:

1. A **WHO/ECDD critical review or treatment guideline** for MDMA, psilocybin, or ibogaine as medicines.
2. A **Celidwen- or UNDRIP-operational protocol** that FDA, EMA, TGA, or Health Canada must run before a botanical or method-of-use PAT application.
3. **Nagoya/PIC/MAT for synthetic analogs and for intangible ceremonial knowledge** (playlists, touch manuals, “inner healer”).
4. **Peyote conservation as a medical-regulator duty** (DEA registers distributors; FDA/WHO do not model harvest pressure from a future mescaline NDA).
5. **Cross-border religious sacrament** under 1971 Art. 32(4) (structurally excluded).
6. **Religious vs medical dual-use of the same molecule in one person** (NAC member in a VA trial; Santo Daime member on COMP360).
7. **Command influence, deployability, PRP/flight status, security-clearance, and disability-rating** aftermath of PAT.
8. **Sponsor-donated Schedule I product + sponsor training inside a national health system** (VA–Compass PIVOT).
9. **AE valence:** when a “positive” psychedelic effect is simultaneously abuse-potential signal, putative mechanism, and reportable AE.
10. **Functional unblinding** as a named Helsinki/CIOMS design problem.
11. **Therapist-investigator dual role** beyond generic COI (who receives a sexual-misconduct complaint: PI, sponsor, licensing board, or none).
12. **Session audiovisual records:** extra-protected psychotherapy notes vs research data vs FDA audit substrate vs AI training; right to **refuse recording without losing trial access**.
13. **AI trip-sitters / integration chatbots** as a regulated medical device vs wellness app.
14. **Post-trial and post-approval access** when the intervention is a 6–8 hour two-therapist session, not a pill (Helsinki ¶34 underspecified).
15. **A national or multi-state PAT disciplinary database** (NPDB/NURSYS catch dually licensed clinicians, not Oregon-only facilitators).
16. **An RCT or even a prospective audit** showing that recording, chaperones, or cooling-off **reduce** PAT harm.
17. **Equity floors** (essential-medicine listing; public-payer cost-effectiveness **with** two-therapist opportunity cost; Indigenous enrollment beyond diversity statements).
18. **Host-country + funder-country REC** for Mexico/Caribbean ibogaine data used to justify US veteran policy.

### Where EVIDENCE is absent

- **No RCT** (and no located prospective outcome study) showing that session recording, chaperones, two-person teams, or cooling-off periods reduce PAT sexual misconduct or other harm. Rationale is deterrence, analogy, and documented failures.
- **No PAT-specific disciplinary database** in the US, Canada, Australia, or New Zealand. Oregon’s 22 orders are the only public numbered series; they are mostly not sexual-misconduct adjudications.
- **TGA zero AP-scheme AEs** through Dec 2025 with tiny n (123 MDMA / 65 psilocybin patients reported) is a **spontaneous-reporting empty**, not a safety finding.
- Colorado has **no public EMS/AE year-end table** comparable to Oregon and **no published licensed-facilitator discipline**.
- **No tried-to-verdict US PAT malpractice** located in this pass; ketamine sexual-assault **criminal** cases exist.
- Whether psychotherapy is **necessary** for MDMA/psilocybin (FDA CRL factorial ask) remains **OPEN scientifically**.
- Functional-unblinding “fixes” (naive-only recruitment, extra deception) can collide with Helsinki ¶26/¶33; **no WMA/CIOMS design document**.

### What would be needed for a credible national or international medical-psychedelic ethics code

This repository is **not** writing that code. A credible instrument would need **process** and **substance**.

**Process.** Multi-profession drafting (psychiatry, psychology, nursing, pharmacy, social work, counseling, addiction medicine — not a single sponsor or adult-use board). **Indigenous co-draft** with FPIC, not a consultation appendix (Celidwen eight Rs as a starting demand, not a brand). Separate tracks for **medical PAT**, **adult-use facilitation**, and **religious/sacramental** use, with an explicit **firewall** so one form does not serve all three. Public comment, conflict-of-interest registers, and a plan for **enforcement** (licensing boards, not only membership expulsion). Veteran/military dual-loyalty review with VA/DoD and independent veterans’ orgs. Nagoya/ABS counsel for any botanical or traditional-knowledge-derived method. Translation into FDA/EMA/TGA/Health Canada **label and REMS language** if a product is approved — a voluntary society code that never becomes a board rule will not close Cover Story’s accountability hole.

**Necessary elements (floors, not this project’s text):**

- Indication honesty; Track A (monitored dosing) vs Track B (licensed psychotherapy); no ballot-box medicine.
- **Drug-class floors:** ibogaine cardiac ≠ ketamine sedation/diversion ≠ classic suggestibility ≠ MDMA hyperthermia/hyponatremia.
- Enhanced consent (identity-level change; Smith & Sisti 2021); freeze on optional permissions after dosing; independent consent-taker in dependent relationships (Helsinki ¶27) — including **command influence**.
- **Sexual-boundary clock** at the strictest medical-board pole for medical PAT (lifetime / never with current and former patients in the MAPS/CRPO sense), with a **stated** rule for dual-licensed adult-use facilitators rather than silent collision.
- Three-tier **touch**; written pre-dose body map; in-session stop always wins.
- **Video:** purpose (fidelity vs safety audit vs AI), retention, patient access, right to refuse without losing care, who watches.
- **AE valence:** capture euphoric/“positive” effects as AEs for abuse-potential **and** as possible mechanism; independent (not PI-only) reporting.
- Dual-role disclosure (prescriber-therapist; therapist-investigator; sponsor-trained interventionist); complaint path **outside** the building.
- Aftercare and non-abandonment, including post-trial access when the “drug” is a two-person day.
- Religious vs medical firewall; peyote/iboga/toad conservation; benefit-sharing where genetic resources or TK are used.
- Veteran coercion, disability-rating, and security-clearance spillover.
- No take-home classic psychedelic; pharmacist stop; at-home ketamine as a named contested class.
- Competence **without** mandatory personal use; integration is not an unregulated upsell label.

Until some body with licensing teeth adopts an instrument like that, the ethical floor for licensed professionals remains **existing medical, psychology, nursing, and pharmacy codes** plus molecule-specific law — not a psychedelic-specific national code.

## Decision rule when capacity is reduced

| Patient state | What the clinician may do | What the clinician may not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before dosing, capacitous | Bind optional preferences (touch, music, visitors, metaphysics-neutrality, recording) | Hide experimental status, alternatives, or conflicts |
| During acute effect | Safety touch, emergency care, honor stop signals, follow the pre-dose plan | Add sexual, financial, or new optional-touch agreements; recruit for other products; impose a belief system |
| After the session, recovered | Revisit preferences, obtain new consent for a later session, offer or refer integration | Treat in-session utterances as durable legal or sexual consent |
