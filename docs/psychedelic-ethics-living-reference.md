# [00] Psychedelic medicine ethics: living reference

Version 1.0. Owner: Blake Reagan (CatCorner22/Ethics). Scope: the ethics, consent, safety and regulatory rules that this project's research proposes for licensed professionals (physicians, NPs, PAs, psychiatrists, psychologists, nurses, pharmacists, psychotherapists) who dispense or support psychedelic medicine in the USA, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, with or without talk therapy; it does not cover recreational use, underground practice guidance, or dosing advice, and it is not legal or medical advice.

Stale-after: 60

The living-reference skill generates the text between each `start` marker and its `end` marker. Regenerate after every edit. Do not edit generated text by hand.

> **Warning.** This file condenses a research synthesis. It is not a professional code, a clinical guideline, or an authorization to possess, prescribe or administer controlled substances. Law, labels and trial status change quickly; confirm every regulatory line against the current primary source and your own college or board before acting.

## [01] Attack sheet

- Name the legal frame (FDA trial, SAP, TGA AP, Medsafe named doctor, Oregon adult use, off-label ketamine, sacramental exemption) in one sentence before anything else. §10.01
- As of 2026-11-11 no classic psychedelic or MDMA is FDA-approved; filings, vouchers and breakthrough labels are not approvals. §60.01
- Say in writing whether the service is Track A (monitored dosing) or Track B (psychotherapy), and bill only what occurred. §10.06, §10.09
- Get consent while the patient has capacity, in stages, before ingestion. §20.01
- After ingestion the patient may tighten limits; the clinician may never loosen them. Intoxicated speech is never sexual or financial consent. §20.02
- Disclose possible lasting change in values, beliefs and relationships. §20.03
- Touch has 3 tiers: safety always, supportive only by written pre-dose agreement, sexual never. §30.01
- Apply the strictest sexual-boundary clock held by anyone on the team. §30.02
- No selling, recruiting, testimonials or will changes in the suggestible window. §30.03
- Two trained persons for hours-long sessions, at least one licensed. §30.05
- If you record sessions, someone must actually watch the recordings. §30.06
- One named most-responsible clinician carries the dosing day. §40.02
- No take-home psychedelic supply; the pharmacist is an independent stop. §40.03
- Match staff and equipment to the molecule: ibogaine needs continuous ECG, ketamine is sedation. §40.04, §40.05, §40.06
- Plan aftercare before the first session; contact within 24 hours. §50.01
- Warn that insurers, employers, compensation boards and courts may compel psychedelic notes. §50.02
- Tell patients how to complain outside the clinic, to the college or board. §50.03
- Personal psychedelic use is not a credential. §50.04
- Treat boundary violations as a foreseeable misuse of the mechanism, not a rogue event. §70.03

## [10] Legal frame and scope of service

### [10.01] Name the legal frame in one sentence before offering any service

<!-- index: legal frame | regulatory pathway; frame test; see also: frame blurring -->

- **Rule.** Before any psychedelic service, the clinician must be able to state in 1 sentence which legal frame authorizes it, without mixing frames. [S1 at Pass 4, track architecture test (i)]
- **Elements.** Frames in use as of 2026-11-11: FDA investigational trial (IND); Health Canada Special Access Program (SAP) or trial; TGA Authorised Prescriber (Australia); Medsafe named-practitioner authorisation (New Zealand); Oregon or Colorado state adult-use or natural-medicine program; off-label racemic ketamine or labelled esketamine; sacramental exemption (RFRA, s.56 CDSA).
- **Test.** Write the sentence. If it needs "and also" across 2 frames, stop and separate the services.
- **Policy.** Each frame carries different consent, staffing and claims rules; confusion is the root of most frame violations.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [10.02] Do not blur medical, sacramental and adult-use frames

<!-- index: frame blurring | clinic as church; church as clinic; see also: religious exemption, legal frame -->

- **Rule.** A licensed clinician who diagnoses and dispenses does not become a church, and a church or adult-use exemption does not authorize medical claims or off-label psychiatric treatment. [S1 at concept 23; S44]
- **Elements.** Native American Church peyote, UDV and Santo Daime ayahuasca, Oregon and Colorado adult use, and licensed medical care are separate legal and ethical systems.
- **Test.** Refer across frames; do not launder one into another (for example, adding a chapel to a clinic, or billing an adult-use session as treatment).
- **Examples.** Oregon dual-licensed facilitators may bring clinical skills only into preparation and integration; administration stays adult-use and non-directive. §60.04
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [10.03] Offer a psychedelic only for a documented indication and name the evidence grade

<!-- index: indication honesty | evidence grade; least-misleading frame; off-label disclosure -->

- **Rule.** Offer a psychedelic only for a documented clinical indication after assessing reasonable alternatives, and say plainly whether use is approved, off-label, investigational, trial or SAP. [S1 at concept 1; S42]
- **Elements.** Name the evidence grade. CANMAT 2023 still treats serotonergic psychedelics as experimental for major depression.
- **Exceptions.** Mystical or personality change may be presented as a benefit only if it is part of the disclosed, evidence-based indication.
- Last verified: 2026-11-05

### [10.04] Prescribe for diagnosable conditions with impairment, not for enhancement

<!-- index: treatment not enhancement | worried well; self-improvement; harm-reduction talk -->

- **Rule.** Prescribe only for a diagnosable condition with functional impairment; do not medicalize ordinary stress or productivity and creativity quests. [S1 at concept 19; S43]
- **Exceptions.** If a patient will use illicitly, discuss adulteration, setting and emergency plans, but do not procure, dose or supervise illegal sessions.
- **Policy.** American Psychiatric Association guidance bars prescribing for self-improvement or recreation.
- Last verified: 2026-11-02

### [10.05] Hold equipoise: legalization, designations and filings are not evidence

<!-- index: clinical equipoise | ballot-box medicine; hype; see also: evidence grade -->

- **Rule.** Do not treat breakthrough designations, priority vouchers, NDA filings, media coverage or state legalization as proof of efficacy; until approval for the indication, default to research or explicitly labelled off-label or SAP use. [S1 at concept 15; S7]
- **Examples.** Compass's COMP360 NDA (completed 2026-11-09) and the MDMA resubmission are filings, not approvals. §60.01
- **Policy.** Health Canada's Scientific Advisory Committee wants established options (including ECT, rTMS, esketamine) tried first. §60.07
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [10.06] Declare in writing whether the service is Track A or Track B

<!-- index: Track A and Track B | two-track architecture; service description -->

- **Rule.** Every service states in its consent and public description whether it is Track A (medically monitored dosing without talk therapy) or Track B (psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy). [S1 at Pass 1, two-track architecture; S6 at section 10]
- **Elements.** Both tracks share one ethical floor (indication, screening, consent, monitoring, rescue, discharge, follow-up). Track B adds a named licensed psychotherapeutic method.
- **Exceptions.** Oregon dual licensees occupy a hybrid: Track A behaviour at administration, limited Track B-like work in preparation and integration only. §60.04
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [10.07] Track A does not include unlicensed psychotherapy

<!-- index: Track A | monitored dosing; no talk therapy -->

- **Rule.** In Track A, acute-session speech is limited to safety, orientation and pre-agreed comfort; do not run trauma processing, regression or meaning-making the clinician is not contracted and licensed to provide. [S1 at concept 6]
- **Elements.** Offer a written list of independent integration resources. Brief medical follow-up (sleep, mood, suicidality, blood pressure) is required and is monitoring, not psychotherapy.
- **Examples.** The College of Alberta Psychologists' silent, non-directive dosing window is a college-level version of Track A behaviour. [S41]
- Last verified: 2026-11-05

### [10.08] Track B uses a named licensed method, two people and independent training

<!-- index: Track B | psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy; PAT -->

- **Rule.** In Track B, preparation, dosing support and integration follow a named method under a therapy consent, with 2 trained persons during dosing and training not supplied solely by a product sponsor. [S1 at concept 7; S11]
- **Elements.** Dual prescriber-therapist roles need disclosure and extra supervision. Consented video, stored as a health record, is a legitimate abuse deterrent only if reviewed. §30.06
- **Open.** The FDA's 2024 MDMA complete response letter left open whether psychotherapy is necessary at all; do not assume the MAPS therapist dyad is the approvable model. §60.02
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [10.09] Name support or psychotherapy and bill only what occurred

<!-- index: support vs psychotherapy | psychological support; billing -->

- **Rule.** State in consent whether the dosing window is psychological support (safety, presence, non-directive) or psychotherapy (a named method with interpretive techniques), and do not bill psychotherapy for an hour of monitoring. [S1 at concept 21; S52]
- **Test.** Would a video audit show only what was billed? Use observable behaviours (Richard and Levine microskills), not branding.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

## [20] Consent and capacity

### [20.01] Obtain consent while the patient has capacity, in stages, before ingestion

<!-- index: informed consent, timing | staged consent; consent process; see also: consent freeze -->

- **Rule.** Give written information at screening, discuss it at a preparation visit, and reconfirm on dosing day before ingestion; never take first-time consent on the dosing day under time or payment pressure. [S6 at section 1; S1 at concept 2]
- **Elements.** Content: indication and evidence grade; regulatory status; alternatives including conventional care and no treatment; conflicts and fees; time course; physical and psychiatric risks; possible lasting change in values; possible non-response; suggestibility; who is in the room; recording and touch policy; limits of confidentiality.
- **Test.** Teach-back, a copy for the patient, and a stated right to withdraw before and during the session.
- **Open.** Preparation also primes expectancy; name that tension in the consent. Colorado's per-session consent detail is marked [verify] in S6.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [20.02] After ingestion the patient may tighten restrictions but the clinician may not loosen them

<!-- index: consent freeze | capacity rule; no expanding consent after dosing; intoxicated consent -->

- **Rule.** Bind optional preferences (touch, visitors, recording, music, metaphysics-neutrality) before dosing; during the acute effect the clinician may only follow the pre-dose plan and give safety care, and intoxicated speech is never later treated as sexual, financial or legal consent. [S1 at decision rule when capacity is reduced; S35]
- **Elements.** In-session "stop" always wins. A pre-dose refusal of supportive touch is not overridden because the intoxicated patient asks.
- **Exceptions.** True emergency or safety care.
- **Examples.** McHerron et al. 2025 found flexible mid-session consent can slide into boundary drift.
- Last verified: 2026-11-05

### [20.03] Disclose unknowable and identity-level effects

<!-- index: enhanced consent | identity change; transformative experience disclosure; see also: transformative consent debate -->

- **Rule.** Tell patients they may not be able to imagine the experience or its effect on their values, politics, metaphysics, religion or relationships, and that they may get no benefit. [S1 at concept 16; S32; S33]
- **Elements.** Let patients who fear identity-level change opt out. Use testimony and education, not hype.
- **Policy.** Consent stays possible when the patient understands they seek change from a bad status quo. §70.04
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [20.04] Protective action during reduced capacity is limited to preventing serious harm

<!-- index: impaired patient | diminished capacity; ABA 1.14 analog -->

- **Rule.** Keep treating the intoxicated patient as the decision-maker for preferences already expressed, and act protectively only to prevent reasonably certain death, serious bodily harm or serious self-harm. [S1 at concept 12; S39]
- **Policy.** "They are not themselves" is not a licence to pursue the clinician's preferred narrative of healing.
- Last verified: 2026-11-02

### [20.05] Use an independent consent-taker when the patient depends on the clinician

<!-- index: independent consent-taker | dependent relationship; Helsinki -->

- **Rule.** Where the patient depends on the clinician (trial participants, carceral, employment-linked or military settings), consent is taken by someone independent. [S53; S6 at section 10]
- Last verified: 2026-11-02

### [20.06] Special populations need extra gates

<!-- index: special populations | adolescents; prisoners; end of life; group dosing; couples; military -->

- **Rule.** Adolescents only with exceptional benefit and paediatric expertise; prisoners only with independent consent and no sentence incentive; end-of-life consent early while capacitous; groups get confidentiality agreements and pairing rules; couples work names who the client is. [S1 at concept 20 and Pass 2 special populations]
- **Elements.** Military patients are told what command can learn.
- Last verified: 2026-11-02

## [30] Boundaries, touch and influence

### [30.01] Use three-tier touch with written pre-dose body-map consent

<!-- index: touch | therapeutic touch; supportive touch; safety touch; body map -->

- **Rule.** Safety touch (vitals, preventing falls or self-injury) is covered by the general safety plan; supportive touch requires a written pre-dose agreement on where, why, who initiates and stop signals plus in-the-moment consent; sexual touch is never permitted. [S1 at concept 3; S28]
- **Elements.** Sexual touch includes kissing, intimate cuddling, genital, chest or mouth contact, and atypical erogenous zones. Train staff on nonverbal withdrawal cues.
- **Open.** The HOPE statement records divided views on whether supportive touch is ever ethical in a suggestible state.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [30.02] Sexual contact is never therapeutic; apply the strictest cooling-off clock on the team

<!-- index: sexual boundaries | sexual misconduct; post-termination clock; strictest-license rule -->

- **Rule.** Sexual or romantic contact with a patient is never diagnostic or therapeutic, and after termination the team follows the strictest clock of any license in the room. [S38; S37; S57; S1 at concept 20]
- **Elements.** Clocks diverge: Colorado facilitators 2 years; APA psychology about 2 years plus burden; ACA and CRPO 5 years (CRPO: still barred after 5 years if a power imbalance persists); AAMFT and MAPS lifetime.
- **Test.** List every license on the team; adopt the longest clock. Never default to Colorado's 2 years if a CRPO-, AAMFT- or MAPS-bound person is present.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [30.03] Do not solicit money, beliefs, testimonials or will changes in the suggestible window

<!-- index: anti-capture | noetic capture; financial capture; testimonials; undue influence -->

- **Rule.** During and immediately after the acute effect, do not solicit business, donations, investments, media appearances or romance, and do not interpret the experience as proof of the clinician's religion, politics or product. [S1 at concept 4; S29]
- **Elements.** No influence over wills or powers of attorney (CRPO). Clinics disclose their philosophical setting in advance. Chaplaincy codes' non-imposition of doctrine is a useful import.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [30.04] Separate the clinical decision from commercial commitment with a cooling-off interval

<!-- index: cooling-off period | no-sales window; upselling -->

- **Rule.** Allow payment for the evaluation at once, but require a delay (for example 24 to 72 hours) before prepaying multi-session packages, refund before dosing, and no upselling within a set window after a session (for example 7 days). [S6 at section 3; S13]
- **Exceptions.** Emergencies and drugs with narrow fixed protocols.
- **Open.** No psychedelic trial tests cooling-off; support is by analogy (FTC Cooling-Off Rule) and HOPE's financial-capture concern. Delays burden patients with limited access.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [30.05] Staff hours-long dosing sessions with two trained persons

<!-- index: two-person team | chaperone; co-therapist -->

- **Rule.** Use 2 trained persons for hours-long dosing sessions wherever feasible, at least 1 a licensed clinician, and never leave the patient alone with a single facilitator for extended periods. [S6 at section 4; S11]
- **Elements.** Chaperones need training, a stated reporting duty and a peer-level channel independent of the lead clinician. A family member is not a chaperone.
- **Open.** Misconduct occurred in a pair-based MDMA program, so team size does not replace oversight.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [30.06] If sessions are recorded, someone must actually review the recordings

<!-- index: session recording | video; recordings audit -->

- **Rule.** Recording of the active phase needs separate written consent for recording and for storage, a retention period, named reviewers, health-record storage, and a defined review use; an unreviewed recording is theatre. [S6 at section 2; S1 at Pass 4, constraints table]
- **Exceptions.** Patient may ask to pause (for example toileting); log the pause.
- **Examples.** MAPS did not watch its session tapes for years, and the published Phase 2 video showed in-session misconduct. [S48]
- **Open.** Recordings are discoverable by courts and insurers; S6 marks the CAP/CPSA recording wording [verify].
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [30.07] Disclose conflicts of interest against conventional care and volume

<!-- index: conflict of interest | fiduciary disclosure; volume incentives -->

- **Rule.** Before recommending a psychedelic, disclose ownership, revenue share, speaker fees, equity, research funding and dosing-volume incentives that could bias against SSRIs, trauma-focused therapy, ECT or no treatment. [S1 at concept 8; S23]
- Last verified: 2026-11-05

### [30.08] Equity and Indigenous reciprocity are written duties, not branding

<!-- index: Indigenous reciprocity | eight Rs; FPIC; benefit-sharing; cultural appropriation -->

- **Rule.** Do not claim ceremonial authority, patent or enclose traditional medicines, or obstruct lawful Indigenous or religious use; if the practice depends commercially on Indigenous knowledge, put free, prior and informed consent and benefit-sharing in writing. [S1 at concept 10; S30]
- **Elements.** No access restriction by race, gender, disability or ability to pay beyond real safety criteria. Reverence includes source ecosystems (peyote, toad, ayahuasca).
- **Examples.** New Zealand's marae-based rongoā trial builds Indigenous protocol into funded trial design; Oregon tourism is not reciprocity. [S26]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

## [40] Medical safety and drug-class floors

### [40.01] Complete medical and psychiatric screening before dosing and never deprescribe casually

<!-- index: screening | contraindications; medication review -->

- **Rule.** Medical and psychiatric assessment, medication and supplement review, and a contraindication screen precede every dosing; other psychotropics are not stopped without a documented plan. [S1 at concept 5; S45]
- **Elements.** Screen for psychosis spectrum, bipolar I or first-degree family history, active suicidality, cardiovascular and hepatic status, pregnancy, serotonergic and MAOI drugs, lithium, QT-prolonging drugs, CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 interactions.
- **Test.** Discharge only when oriented and steady; no driving or high-stakes decisions that day.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [40.02] A named most-responsible clinician carries the dosing day

<!-- index: most-responsible clinician | medical director; three-role split; see also: virtual care -->

- **Rule.** A licensed clinician competent in the drug's medical risks is responsible for the dosing day; a virtual prescriber plus unlicensed sitters fails college standards for IV, IM or high-dose work. [S1 at concept 17; S24]
- **Elements.** Ketamine three-role split: mental-health professional, medical professional, patient; one person holds both professional roles only if truly qualified in both. Nurses keep independent reporting duties.
- **Examples.** An Ontario family physician was ordered to stop IV and IM ketamine for unapproved premises, thin psychiatric documentation and improper virtual delegation.
- Last verified: 2026-11-05

### [40.03] No take-home psychedelic supply; the pharmacist is an independent stop

<!-- index: take-home dosing | home use; diversion; pharmacist checkpoint -->

- **Rule.** Do not dispense classic psychedelics or MDMA for unsupervised home use; pharmacists refuse prescriptions that are recreational, enhancement-seeking or outside the prescriber's scope. [S1 at concept 18; S22]
- **Elements.** Locked storage, counts and reconciliation; Spravato-style supervised administration where it fits.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [40.04] Match staff, equipment and exclusions to the molecule

<!-- index: drug-class risk floor | molecule-specific safety; see also: ibogaine, ketamine, MDMA -->

- **Rule.** Ibogaine is not ketamine and ketamine is not a classic psychedelic; staff, equipment and exclusion criteria follow the molecule, not a generic "journey" protocol. [S1 at concept 22; S5 at cross-drug comparison]
- **Elements.** Main emergency by class: classic psychedelics, severe agitation and hypertensive spike; MDMA, hyperthermia, hyponatraemia, arrhythmia; ketamine, respiratory depression and blood-pressure spike; ayahuasca, serotonin syndrome and aspiration; ibogaine, QT prolongation and torsades.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [40.05] Ibogaine requires continuous ECG in a setting that can treat torsades

<!-- index: ibogaine | noribogaine; QT prolongation; torsades de pointes -->

- **Rule.** Ibogaine and noribogaine need continuous ECG with QTc tracking for the whole acute period, corrected potassium and magnesium, CYP2D6 caution, and a team that can defibrillate and treat torsades; microdosing does not waive cardiac screening. [S1 at concept 22; S8; S55]
- **Elements.** Deaths have occurred under medical supervision and in people without known heart disease. Opioid-dependent patients face overdose risk after treatment because tolerance falls; arrange naloxone and continuing addiction care.
- **Policy.** Cardiac risk is now regulator language (FDA RFI 2026-10-05), not only NGO commentary. §60.03
- **Open.** S5's ibogaine status in Canada is marked [verify].
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [40.06] Treat ketamine as sedation with diversion control

<!-- index: ketamine | racemic ketamine; compounded ketamine; parenteral ketamine -->

- **Rule.** Monitor ketamine patients for dissociation and post-dose sedation, not vitals alone, give IV ketamine only in a hospital or accredited facility where the college requires it, and run diversion controls. [S20; S1 at Pass 3, CPSBC ketamine]
- **Elements.** In-clinic administration where possible; counts, locked storage, prescription-monitoring checks; no bulk prescribing to patients with misuse history.
- **Examples.** Matthew Perry's death followed a clinic's refusal to escalate and his turn to illicit supply; FDA warned about compounded ketamine in 2023. [S3 at category J]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [40.07] MDMA sessions need two persons and temperature and sodium vigilance

<!-- index: MDMA | midomafetamine; MDMA-assisted therapy -->

- **Rule.** MDMA dosing needs 2 trained persons, frequent blood pressure, heart rate and temperature checks, hydration without overhydration, and overnight stay or accompanied discharge per protocol. [S5 at section 2]
- **Elements.** Exclude uncontrolled hypertension, arrhythmia or QT prolongation, hepatic impairment, pregnancy; MAOI combination is dangerous.
- **Open.** Drafted from memory; S5 marks the CRL date [verify].
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [40.08] Classic psychedelics need psychiatric rescue and staffing for long sessions

<!-- index: psilocybin | LSD; mescaline; classic psychedelics -->

- **Rule.** For psilocybin (about 6 to 8 hours), LSD and mescaline (about 10 to 12 hours), provide continuous presence, interval vitals, a written psychiatric rescue plan and staffing that covers the full effect plus its tail. [S5 at sections 1, 4 and 8; S45]
- **Elements.** Lithium is a contraindication in many protocols. Do not copy trial doses outside a protocol.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [40.09] Ayahuasca and DMT require MAOI interaction screening and a standardized product

<!-- index: ayahuasca | DMT; MAOI interaction -->

- **Rule.** Screen ayahuasca patients for serotonergic and sympathomimetic interactions with its MAOI component, protect the airway during vomiting, and in medical settings use only an analytically standardized product. [S5 at section 5]
- **Open.** S5 marks Canadian DMT scheduling and Colorado timing [verify].
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [40.10] 5-MeO-DMT requires readiness for abrupt onset and a synthetic source

<!-- index: 5-MeO-DMT | mebufotenin; toad venom -->

- **Rule.** Prepare for sudden loss of control, vomiting and hypertension within minutes of 5-MeO-DMT, and prefer synthetic product over toad-derived material. [S5 at section 6]
- **Policy.** A short session is not a weak suggestibility window. [S1 at Pass 4, pipeline table]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

## [50] Continuity, records and accountability

### [50.01] Plan aftercare before the first session and contact within 24 hours

<!-- index: aftercare | integration; follow-up; post-trial care; non-abandonment -->

- **Rule.** Psychedelic care is a course, not a session: plan post-course care before the first dose, contact the patient within 24 hours and again within about a week, and terminate only with referral. [S1 at concept 13; S6 at section 7; S36; S56]
- **Elements.** Screen for suicidality (for example the Columbia scale), mania, psychosis, sleep and relational rupture; give a 24-hour contact route.
- **Examples.** Cover Story's "open-heart surgery, then walked away with the chest still open" is the failure mode to plan against. [S46]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [50.02] Treat session content as health information and warn about compelled records

<!-- index: confidentiality | compelled records; privacy; subpoena -->

- **Rule.** Session content, including spiritual material, is health information, and before the patient speaks in a charted session they are warned that insurers, employers, workers' compensation boards and courts may compel the notes. [S1 at concept 9; S41]
- **Elements.** Recordings are not marketing assets. Group members sign confidentiality agreements.
- Last verified: 2026-11-05

### [50.03] Give patients a complaint route that leaves the building

<!-- index: complaints | accountability; external reporting; adverse events -->

- **Rule.** Tell patients at intake how to complain to the clinic and directly to the licensing college or board and, where relevant, FDA or Health Canada; no non-disparagement clauses and no retaliation. [S1 at concept 14; S6 at section 8]
- **Elements.** Named complaint person independent of the treating clinician; annual review of adverse events and boundary incidents; external, not sponsor-only, safety reporting.
- **Policy.** Internal channels failed in a trial setting; FDA advisers asked for external reporting. [S10]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [50.04] Competence does not require personal psychedelic use

<!-- index: competence | training; personal use; change of scope -->

- **Rule.** Document training in pharmacology, emergencies, consent, boundaries and (for Track B) the therapy method; personal psychedelic experience is neither required nor a credential, and clinicians never dose while providing the service. [S1 at concept 11; S28]
- **Elements.** Clinicians entering the field complete change-of-scope where the college requires it.
- **Policy.** Requiring personal use is also an ableist exclusion. [S3 at category P]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [50.05] Give each team member a written scope and regular supervision

<!-- index: supervision | consultation; scope of practice -->

- **Rule.** Each team member has a written scope, there is regular clinical supervision or peer consultation for boundary dilemmas, and new practitioners co-treat under someone experienced with similar patients. [S6 at section 6; S25]
- **Open.** Qualified supervisors are scarce.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [50.06] Advertise only evidence-grade claims and state fees fairly

<!-- index: advertising and fees | marketing; uninsured fees -->

- **Rule.** Advertising uses outcome ranges and evidence grades, never testimonials obtained in a suggestible window, and uninsured prices are stated clearly before commitment. [S1 at concept 24; S20]
- **Examples.** A clinic marketing "coming FDA approval" fails honesty. §60.01
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [50.07] Virtual care cannot replace in-person dosing accountability

<!-- index: virtual care | telehealth; remote supervision -->

- **Rule.** Virtual preparation and integration may widen access, but dosing requires in-person accountability by a responsible clinician. [S1 at concept 24]
- Last verified: 2026-11-05

### [50.08] Protect provider capacity with caseload limits and no impaired practice

<!-- index: provider wellbeing | burnout; provider impairment -->

- **Rule.** Set caseload and session-length limits, debrief after difficult sessions, and bar services while impaired. [S6 at section 9; S49]
- **Open.** Direct evidence is thin; "wellbeing" must not become a route to mandatory personal drug use. S6 marks its burnout citation [verify].
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

## [60] Regulatory status as of 2026-11-11

### [60.01] No classic psychedelic or MDMA is FDA-approved; they remain Schedule I

<!-- index: FDA approval status | Schedule I; COMP360; NDA; see also: esketamine -->

- **Rule.** As of 2026-11-11 FDA has not approved MDMA, psilocybin, LSD, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, ibogaine, mescaline or 2C-B, and each stays Schedule I until FDA approval plus DEA rescheduling. [S2 at 1.1; S7; S9; S12]
- **Elements.** 2026 policy stack (executive and agency, not approvals): 18 April executive order; 24 April priority vouchers (psilocybin TRD and MDD, methylone PTSD) and a noribogaine Phase 1 IND; July final guidance on psychedelic trials; September Part 15 hearing; 5 October ibogaine RFI.
- **Examples.** COMP360 NDA completed 2026-11-09 (COMPANY); launch would still need rescheduling. Usona's filing is trade-press reported, not FDA-confirmed.
- **Open.** Re-check NDA acceptance and PDUFA dates on FDA.gov.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [60.02] The MDMA complete response letter is the template US reviewers will apply

<!-- index: MDMA complete response letter | CRL; PDAC 2024; Lykos -->

- **Rule.** Any US psychedelic program and any ethics code must assume the 2024 MDMA CRL concerns are live: unreliable adverse-event capture, short durability, expectancy and prior-use bias, unaudited session recordings, and whether psychotherapy is necessary. [S11; S10; S2 at 1.4]
- **Elements.** PDAC on 2024-06-04 voted 9 to 2 that efficacy was not shown and 10 to 1 that benefits did not outweigh risks. Resilient resubmitted in August 2026 without a new Phase 3 (COMPANY); FDA acceptance not confirmed.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [60.03] The FDA ibogaine RFI sets a cardiac floor

<!-- index: ibogaine RFI | FDA-2026-N-10429 -->

- **Rule.** FDA's 2026-10-05 ibogaine request for information describes starting doses not above 10 mg/kg, sequential dose ascent, cardiac and neurologic monitoring and independent safety oversight; treat that as the floor. [S8; S2 at 1.2]
- **Open.** Comments close 2026-11-20; re-check for any resulting guidance.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [60.04] Oregon is supported adult use, not medical prescribing

<!-- index: Oregon | Oregon Psilocybin Services; HB 2387; dual licensure -->

- **Rule.** Oregon psilocybin services are state-licensed adult use (age 21 and over, non-directive facilitation, no take-home, sessions end by midnight); HB 2387 lets dual-licensed facilitators provide health services only in preparation and integration. [S14; S15; S2 at section 2]
- **Elements.** 2025: 5,935 clients with administration sessions, 13 EMS contacts (0.2%), nearly half of data-sharing clients from out of state.
- **Exceptions.** A facilitator must not consume product while serving.
- **Open.** Low EMS counts are not pharmacovigilance and miss delayed psychological harm.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [60.05] Colorado facilitators: consent each session and a two-year sexual clock

<!-- index: Colorado | natural medicine; 4 CCR 755-1 -->

- **Rule.** Colorado facilitator rules require informed consent each session, a touch contract, no dual-authority relationships, no services while impaired, no referral fees, a 2-year post-termination sexual ban, and the stricter code for dual licensees. [S51; S1 at Pass 1]
- Last verified: 2026-11-02

### [60.06] Texas funds ibogaine research only; psilocybin bills died; ketamine rules are proposed

<!-- index: Texas | SB 2308; Texas ibogaine; TMB ketamine rules -->

- **Rule.** Texas SB 2308 (LAW, signed 2025-06-11) funds FDA-path ibogaine trials and is not legal ibogaine treatment; HB 4014 and HB 4813 died in the Senate; the Medical Board's parenteral ketamine rules (clinic registration, on-site monitoring, no home parenteral use) are PROPOSAL only. [S16; S17; S18]
- **Open.** Confirm the Texas Register before treating the ketamine rules as binding; statute may still require a private match.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [60.07] In Canada SAP access is not an efficacy finding and the advisory committee is a brake

<!-- index: Canada | Special Access Program; SAP; Health Canada SAC -->

- **Rule.** A Health Canada SAP authorization is not a quality or efficacy finding, patients may not possess or transport the drug, and the 2025 to 2026 Scientific Advisory Committee consensus limits investigational psychedelic therapy to patients who have exhausted established options. [S19; S21; S2 at section 4]
- **Elements.** SAC: psychiatrist involvement strongly recommended; psychiatric emergencies do not belong in outpatient psychedelic clinics; direct-to-consumer marketing of experimental drugs is a concern.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [60.08] Alberta licenses psychedelic facilities and its psychologists do not recommend drugs

<!-- index: Alberta | CAP PAPT guideline; Alberta standards -->

- **Rule.** Alberta is the only province with a licensed psychedelic treatment facility regime, and College of Alberta Psychologists members do not diagnose the need for or recommend a specific psychedelic and need 5 years on the general register before offering psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy. [S40; S41]
- **Elements.** Integration within 1 to 3 days, non-directive; medical questions go to the prescriber.
- Last verified: 2026-11-05

### [60.09] Australia limits prescribing to authorised psychiatrists; the May 2026 changes are recommendations

<!-- index: Australia | TGA Authorised Prescriber; RANZCP -->

- **Rule.** Since 2023-07-01 MDMA for PTSD and psilocybine for TRD are Schedule 8 only when prescribed by a TGA Authorised Prescriber psychiatrist after ethics-committee approval; the products remain unapproved and take-home is not anticipated. [S22; S2 at section 5]
- **Elements.** The 2026-05-26 TGA recommendations (on-site AP at administration, wider therapy dyad) are PROPOSAL, not regulation. [S25]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [60.10] New Zealand authorises named practitioners only

<!-- index: New Zealand | Medsafe; named-practitioner authorisation -->

- **Rule.** New Zealand has no approved psychedelic medicine; outside trials, prescribing requires named-practitioner authorisation, first granted on 2025-06-19 to one Christchurch psychiatrist for TRD, and officials do not recommend law change. [S26; S27]
- **Exceptions.** RANZCP guidance does not create New Zealand prescribing rights.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [60.11] Religious exemptions do not authorize medical practice

<!-- index: religious exemption | RFRA; UDV; s.56 exemption; peyote -->

- **Rule.** US federal peyote protection for the Native American Church, RFRA protection for sincere religious ayahuasca use, and Health Canada s.56 exemptions for UDV and Santo Daime cover religious practice only, never medical practice. [S44; S1 at Pass 3, religious law]
- **Elements.** RFRA binds federal, not automatically state, law. Canada has no general First Nations exemption for psilocybin or ayahuasca.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [60.12] Esketamine is the only approved psychedelic-adjacent psychiatric drug

<!-- index: esketamine | Spravato; REMS -->

- **Rule.** Esketamine nasal spray is approved for TRD and for depression with suicidal ideation under a REMS with supervised self-administration and at least 2 hours of post-dose observation; racemic ketamine is approved as an anaesthetic and its psychiatric use is off-label. [S2 at 1.1; S54]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

## [70] Evidence quality and criticism

### [70.01] Functional unblinding means psychedelic effect sizes are not ordinary RCT evidence

<!-- index: functional unblinding | expectancy; blinding -->

- **Rule.** Do not quote psychedelic trial effect sizes as if from well-blinded RCTs: about 90% of MDMA and 75% of placebo participants guessed their assignment. [S10; S1 at Pass 2, FDA PDAC]
- **Open.** Fixes (naive-only samples, concealment) raise their own Helsinki issues.
- Last verified: 2026-11-02

### [70.02] Suggestibility is the mechanism of both benefit and harm

<!-- index: suggestibility | REBUS; non-ordinary states; NOSC -->

- **Rule.** If the REBUS model is right, the openness that produces benefit also produces vulnerability, so asking patients to "trust, let go, be open" without disclosing that dependence is unethical. [S31; S4 at A3]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [70.03] Treat boundary violations as foreseeable misuse of the mechanism

<!-- index: Cover Story | Power Trip; foreseeable misuse; see also: sexual boundaries -->

- **Rule.** Design Track B on the assumption that sexual and boundary violations are a foreseeable misuse of long, trusting, touch-permitting sessions, not a rogue one-off. [S46; S47; S48; S1 at Pass 4, media criticisms]
- **Elements.** Video-documented in-session conduct in a MAPS Phase 2 trial; MAPS-acknowledged post-session sexual relationship; MAPS did not review tapes for years.
- **Open.** Many claims in S3 are tagged alleged, not proven; the file does not adjudicate them.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [70.04] Consent is possible if enhanced; the transformative-consent debate stays open

<!-- index: transformative consent debate | transformative experience; L. A. Paul -->

- **Rule.** Treat informed consent to psychedelics as possible with enhanced disclosure, while naming in the consent form that values may change in ways the patient cannot foresee. [S32; S33; S34; S4 at A1]
- **Elements.** Jacobs: disclosure cannot guarantee values-aligned choice. Villiger: aspiration to leave a bad status quo grounds consent. Kious: strict transformation claims lack evidence and would disable much of medicine.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [70.05] Neither the underground nor the clinic is automatically safe

<!-- index: underground vs clinical | clinic is not a safeguard -->

- **Rule.** Do not treat a regulated clinical frame as proof of safety: the leading clinical MDMA program had cameras, ethics review and FDA oversight and still had pinning and sexual contact. [S3 at category G; S46]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [70.06] Regulatory-capture claims run both ways; disclose affiliations

<!-- index: regulatory capture | Psymposia; public comment -->

- **Rule.** Anyone giving public comment or external reports discloses affiliations, because both sponsor capture and undisclosed activist blocs have been alleged around the 2024 PDAC. [S50; S3 at category N]
- **Policy.** The counter-claim does not loosen §30.01, §10.08 or §10.05.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [70.07] Psychedelics are distinctive but get no separate evidentiary standard

<!-- index: psychedelic exceptionalism | Pollan effect; see also: clinical equipoise -->

- **Rule.** Distinctive effects do not justify skipping RCTs or declaring consent impossible. [S1 at journal synthesis theme 9; S36]
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

### [70.08] Practice-level safeguards are untested; treat them as rationale, not proof

<!-- index: practice evidence | safeguard evidence; rationale strength -->

- **Rule.** Almost no process safeguard (recording, chaperones, cooling-off, supervision) has been tested in a trial of practice outcomes; describe them as strength of rationale and audit them prospectively. [S6 at evidence honesty]
- **Open.** Which safeguards reduce harm and which are rituals; how to credential integration providers.
- Last verified: 2026-11-11

## [80] Glossary

- **Track A** (UF: monitored dosing): medically monitored dosing without talk therapy; speech limited to safety and pre-agreed comfort. §10.07
- **Track B** (UF: PAT, psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy): Track A plus a named licensed psychotherapeutic method across preparation, dosing and integration. §10.08
- **Consent freeze** (UF: capacity rule): after ingestion the patient may tighten but the clinician may not loosen pre-dose limits. §20.02
- **Enhanced consent**: disclosure of unknowable, identity-level effects and possible non-response. §20.03
- **NOSC** (UF: non-ordinary state of consciousness): the acute altered state during which capacity and suggestibility shift. §70.02
- **Functional unblinding**: participants can tell drug from placebo, weakening blinding. §70.01
- **REBUS**: relaxed beliefs under psychedelics, a model in which loosened priors drive both benefit and suggestibility. §70.02
- **SAP** (UF: Special Access Program): Health Canada case-by-case access route; not an efficacy finding. §60.07
- **Authorised Prescriber** (UF: AP): TGA status letting a named psychiatrist prescribe MDMA or psilocybine. §60.09
- **CRL** (UF: complete response letter): FDA refusal to approve as submitted, listing deficiencies. §60.02
- **PDAC**: FDA Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee. §60.02
- **REMS**: FDA Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy. §60.12
- **FPIC**: free, prior and informed consent of Indigenous communities. §30.08
- **Strictest-license rule**: the team follows the most demanding boundary rule held by any member. §30.02
- **Most-responsible clinician** (UF: MRC): the licensed clinician accountable for the dosing day. §40.02
