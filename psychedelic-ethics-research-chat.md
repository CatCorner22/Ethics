## How to read this document

Licensed psychedelic care should keep the ethical **floor of medicine and law** (honest indication, capacitous consent, non-exploitation, safety, confidentiality) and add duties ordinary prescribing does not cover: hours-long reduced autonomy, suggestibility, touch, and a clear choice between **monitored dosing (Track A)** and **psychotherapy (Track B)**.

## Pass 1 — Core professional codes and first proposal

### Medical ethics (USA / Canada)

**AMA Code of Medical Ethics** (Principles revised 2001; opinions through 2024). Nine principles: competence and compassion; professionalism and honesty; respect for law; rights and confidentiality; science and consultation; freedom of practice (except emergencies); public health; patient paramount; access.

Opinion clusters that matter for dispensing: patient–physician relationship (trust, non-abandonment, sexual/romantic prohibition, industry influence); consent, communication, decision making (capacity, documentation — Opinion 2.1.1, 2024); privacy and electronic records (minimum-necessary, harm exceptions, AMA 3.3.2); research and innovation; financing; self-regulation.

**CMA Code of Ethics and Professionalism (2018).** Virtues: compassion, honesty, humility, integrity, prudence. Commitments: well-being of the patient; respect for persons (never exploit); justice; integrity and competence; collaboration; self-care; inquiry and reflection.

**Belmont Report.** Respect for persons (autonomy plus extra protection when autonomy is reduced); beneficence; justice.

### Psychological therapy ethics

**APA Ethics Code (2017).** Principles: Beneficence and Nonmaleficence; Fidelity and Responsibility; Integrity; Justice; Respect for People’s Rights and Dignity. Ten enforceable sections, including Therapy 10.01(b) extra disclosure for unestablished techniques; sexual intimacies with current clients banned; former clients restricted (typically 2+ years plus burden on the psychologist).

**CPA Code (4th ed., 2017).** Weighted when principles conflict (except imminent bodily harm): (I) Dignity of Persons and Peoples; (II) Responsible Caring; (III) Integrity in Relationships; (IV) Responsibility to Society. Includes freedom of consent and vulnerable-group protection.

**ACA Code (2014).** Principles: autonomy, nonmaleficence, beneficence, justice, fidelity, veracity. Nine practice sections, including value non-imposition.

Therapy codes go deeper than medical codes on dual relationships, sexual boundaries, transference, and experimental techniques. Those duties do not disappear if the licensed professional chooses medical monitoring instead of psychotherapy.

### Legal ethics (USA / Canada)

**ABA Model Rules.** Eight structural categories. Especially relevant: 1.1 competence; 1.4 communication; 1.6 confidentiality; 1.7–1.8 conflicts (including no sexual relationship with the client); **1.14 diminished capacity** (maintain a normal relationship as far as reasonably possible; protective action only against substantial harm); 1.16 decline/terminate without abandonment; 7.1 advertising.

**FLSC Model Code (amended 2024).** Adopted or aligned in 13 of 14 law societies: integrity; competence; quality of service including impaired decision-making; confidentiality; conflicts and loyalty; fees; withdrawal; marketing; advocacy; discrimination and harassment.

Legal ethics analog: when capacity drops, the professional is not a substitute decision-maker for optional, identity-shaping choices (worldview, sexual contact, new financial agreements). Protective action is limited to safety.

### Psychedelic-specific sources (pass 1)

**MAPS Code of Ethics for Psychedelic Psychotherapy (v4).** Safety; confidentiality; transparency; alliance and trust; touch; sexual boundaries (no sexual contact with participant, former participant, or family—during or after); diversity; non-ordinary states; finances; competence; colleagues; relationship to self.

**McGuire et al., JAMA Network Open (2024) Banbury consensus.** Five issue areas: reparations/reciprocity/equity/respect; informed consent (including non-response); professional boundaries and touch; personal experience **not required** for licensure; gatekeeping (training, insurance, DEA/FDA). Safety touch always; supportive touch with shared decision-making; mid-session refusal honored.

**HOPE statement (AJOB, 2024).** Indigenous position and benefit-sharing; precautionary science; diverse motivations; anti-hype education; consent and divided views on therapeutic touch; equity; professional conduct; suggestibility/noetic/financial capture; interdisciplinary research; communication ethics.

**Alberta Psychedelic Drug Treatment Services Standards (2023).** Licensed medical model: capacity required; full psych/medical assessment; experimental-nature consent; alternatives; conflict-of-interest vs conventional care; therapeutic-touch disclosure; continuous monitoring; medical director accountability; abuse-reporting; controlled-drug security; end-of-life administration processes.

**Health Canada.** SAP is emergency access, not a quality/efficacy endorsement. Trial-risk notices: two therapists during dosing; disclose suggestibility, transference, suicidality, cardiovascular risk.

**Oregon OAR 333-333-5120.** Supported adult use, **not** medical prescribing: client-first duty; no misrepresentation; default nondirective facilitation; emergency recognition; supportive touch only with prior written consent; facilitator must not consume product while serving.

**Colorado 4 CCR 755-1 (2024).** Informed consent each session; touch contract; confidentiality limits; no dual-authority relationships; sexual/romantic ban for **two years** post-termination; no services while impaired; no referral fees; dual-licensees follow the stricter code.

### Shared categories across professions (pass 1)

Twelve families appear in medical, therapy, and legal codes and **intensify** under psychedelics: competence/scope; beneficence/nonmaleficence; autonomy/consent; confidentiality; loyalty/conflicts; sexual/dual relationships; honesty/advertising; justice/access; records/fees/CDS property; impaired client/capacity; teamwork/interruption; self-regulation/reporting.

### Psychedelic-specific gaps (pass 1)

Suggestibility and noetic capture; three-tier touch; whether the clinician must have taken the drug; medical vs adult-use vs ceremonial frames; whether psychotherapy is required; Indigenous knowledge and benefit-sharing; consent language priming set; identity-level after-effects.

### Two-track architecture

**Track A — medically monitored dosing (no talk therapy).** Indication, screening, consent, witnessed dosing, physiologic and behavioral monitoring, rescue, discharge, structured check-in. Acute-session speech limited to safety, orientation, and pre-agreed comfort. Do not run unlicensed trauma processing. Offer independent integration resources. Medical follow-up is monitoring, not PAT.

**Track B — psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy.** All of A plus a named licensed psychotherapeutic method (preparation, dosing-session sitting within a therapy contract, integration). Prefer two trained persons in the room. If prescriber and therapist are the same person, disclose dual role and add oversight.

**Capacity rule.** Before dosing, bind optional preferences. During acute effect: safety plus the pre-dose plan; in-session “stop” always wins. After recovery: new consent for later sessions—never treat intoxicated speech as sexual or financial consent.

### Concepts 1–14 (first proposal)

1. **Indication honesty and least-misleading frame.** Documented clinical indication; name evidence grade; call off-label/investigational/SAP what it is.
2. **Capacitous consent before ingestion, freeze on optional new permissions.** After ingestion the patient may tighten restrictions; clinician may not loosen them except true emergency/safety care.
3. **Three-tier touch.** Safety touch always; supportive touch only with written pre-dose agreement and stop signals; sexual touch never.
4. **Anti-capture: beliefs, money, testimonials.** No soliciting business, donations, investments, media, or romance in the suggestible window.
5. **Medical screening, setting, and rescue as non-delegable duties.**
6. **Track A: do not smuggle in unlicensed psychotherapy.**
7. **Track B: licensed therapy frame, two-person default, extra dual-role caution.**
8. **Fiduciary conflict disclosure against conventional care.**
9. **Confidentiality with NOSC-specific limits.**
10. **Equity, cultural non-imposition, and Indigenous non-interference.**
11. **Competence without mandatory personal use.**
12. **Impaired-patient protective action, limited to harm (ABA 1.14 analog).**
13. **Continuity, non-abandonment, and delayed harm.**
14. **Accountability architecture** (college/board complaint paths, not only internal).

## Pass 2 — Allied licenses, regulators, special populations

### Psychiatry (American Psychiatric Association)

**Position statement:** inadequate scientific evidence for endorsing psychedelics to treat any psychiatric disorder except in approved investigational studies. Clinical treatments should follow evidence and regulatory standards, not ballot initiatives or popular opinion.

**Resource document, Psychedelics in Psychiatry:** research equipoise; functional unblinding; **enhanced consent** (possible change in personality, politics, metaphysics, religion); patient vulnerability and sexual/financial exploitation; **double consent** for touch; no prescribing for self-improvement or recreation; harm-reduction conversations without assisting illegal use; equity and Indigenous ceremonial distinction; avoid ketamine-clinic over-exuberance.

Hard line: pharmacological interventions require a **diagnosable condition and functional impairment**. Ordinary talk therapy for the “worried well” does not license a psychedelic as moral enhancement.

### Nursing

**ANA 2025 (10 provisions):** dignity; primary commitment to care recipient; trust/advocacy/safety; authority and accountability; duties to self and safe workplace; ethical work environment; knowledge and policy; collaboration; social justice; global and environmental health.

**CNA 2025 (7 values):** dignity and autonomy; relationships and humanizing care; integrity and accountability; truth and reconciliation; social justice; competent practice; privacy and confidentiality. Themes: anti-racism, nurse well-being, harm reduction, MAID, digital ethics. Reporting unsafe colleague practice is a duty.

### Pharmacy

**APhA Code (1994, reviewed 2017):** covenantal relationship; patient good and confidentiality; autonomy; honesty; competence; respect for colleagues; individual vs societal needs; justice in resource distribution.

**Ontario College of Pharmacists:** beneficence, nonmaleficence, respect/justice, fidelity; no controlled act for unethical or illegal purpose; prevent and report substance misuse; diversion security and reconciliation.

Pharmacists are an independent ethical checkpoint.

### Social work

**NASW 1.06:** conflicts of interest; no exploitation for personal/religious/political/business ends; dual/multiple relationships; clarify who the client is in couples/families.

**CASW 2024:** maintain boundaries and avoid COI even when “dual relationship” is not named in the Code.

### CRPO (Ontario registered psychotherapy)

Avoid dual relationships with current clients except small-community necessity. No abuse of clients or close others. **No undue influence over wills, powers of attorney, or personal life decisions.** Person remains a client for sexual-abuse purposes for **five years** after termination; after five years still prohibited if power imbalance risks exploitation. RHPA mandatory revocation for specified sexual acts.

For a medicine that can create noetic attachment, licensed medical teams should default to the **strictest** sexual-boundary code held by anyone in the room (often five years +, not Colorado’s two-year facilitator rule).

### FSMB 2020 (physician sexual misconduct)

Sexual behavior between physician and patient is **never diagnostic or therapeutic**. Includes verbal, physical, or virtual conduct a patient could reasonably construe as sexual. A single serious incident may warrant revocation.

### FDA PDAC — 4 June 2024 (Lykos MDMA)

Voted 9–2 data did not show PTSD efficacy; 10–1 benefits did not outweigh risks. Ethics baked into benefit–risk:

- Functional unblinding (~90% on drug, ~75% on placebo guessed assignment)
- Therapist suggestibility; want independent (not sponsor-only) training; two licensed persons
- Documented phase-2 sexual misconduct (therapists in bed with a participant)
- Impairment lasting beyond the session; caution deprescribing other psychotropics
- Incomplete cardiac/QT and lab data; hyponatremia; abuse/diversion
- REMS discussion: medical staff onsite; **external** safety reporting

### Australia — TGA authorised prescribers + RANZCP (from 1 July 2023)

Specialist psychiatrists only. MDMA for PTSD; psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression. NHMRC-registered HREC must approve the protocol before TGA Authorised Prescriber status. RANZCP: established treatments tried without lasting success; psychiatrist overall responsibility; training or close supervision by someone who has treated at least one similar patient. TGA does **not anticipate** take-home dispensing.

### Helsinki 2024

Plain-language consent including funding, COI, incentives, harm compensation; independent consent-takers when the participant is in a dependent relationship; placebo only if methodologically necessary and not exposing people to additional serious irreversible harm.

### Celidwen et al. (2023) — eight Rs

Acknowledgement: Reverence, Respect. Knowledge: Responsibility, Relevance. Intellectual property: Regulation, Reparation. Belonging: Restoration, Reconciliation. Clinic-level: FPIC, no token Indigenous presence, no enclosing patents, benefit-sharing, ecosystem care.

### Transformative-consent debate

Jacobs (2023), using L. A. Paul: if the experience and resulting values cannot be known in advance, standard “understanding” for informed consent fails for values-alignment. Villiger (2024) and later AJOB work: patients often know they want to leave a bad status quo; aspiration can ground consent. APA psychiatry practical synthesis: disclose possible identity-level change; let concerned patients opt out; treat preparation as an extension of consent **and** as set-priming.

| Adolescents | Almost no developmental data; exceptional benefit only |

### Ontario / ketamine (pass 2)

CPSO has no ketamine-specific policy; CAM policy applies (evidence proportionate to risk). OHPIP Level 2 for IV/IM ketamine. ICRC case: family physician ordered to stop IV/IM ketamine without premises approval, inadequate psychiatric documentation, improper **virtual** delegation, change-of-scope required.

### Concepts 15–20 (second proposal)

15. **Clinical equipoise and no ballot-box medicine.**
16. **Enhanced consent for unknowable and identity-level effects.**
17. **Most-responsible clinician present in the dosing-day chain** — not a virtual prescriber plus unlicensed sitters.
18. **No take-home psychedelic; pharmacist as independent stop.**
19. **Treatment not enhancement; harm-reduction talk without illegal assistance.**
20. **Strictest-license sexual boundary and special-population gates.**

## Pass 3 — Canada+, drug-class floors, frames, aftercare

### CANMAT 2023 (serotonergic psychedelics for MDD)

Low-level evidence (3–4); small samples; functional unblinding. Consensus: **experimental**; use primarily in clinical trials; SAP only in rare special circumstances. Protocol details (dose, repeats, psychotherapy type, music, follow-up) remain expert-opinion driven.

### Health Canada Scientific Advisory Committee on Mental Health Disorders (sessions through April 2026)

Investigational therapies, including PAT, only after **exhaustion of established options**; qualified practitioners; specialized settings; prefer research; exceptional access for special circumstances; document that evidence-based treatments were trialed; measurement-based outcome reporting.

### College of Alberta Psychologists — PAPT guideline (30 June 2025)

- Psychologists do **not** diagnose the need for, or recommend, specific psychedelic medicines.
- Minimum **five years** on the general register in trauma/mood evidence-based therapy before offering PAPT.
- Three phases: preparation, administration, integration within 1–3 days (non-directive).
- Dosing window intended **silent and non-directive**.
- Unregulated guides/sitters supervised under CAP’s non-regulated-persons guideline, with the prescriber.
- CPSA recommends **recording** the active phase; written consent for storage and access.
- Medical and psychiatric contraindication lists; medical questions referred to the prescriber.
- Warn that **WCB, insurers, employers, subpoenas** may compel psychedelic notes.

**Support versus psychotherapy.** Goodwin and others argue trials can use psychological support, not therapy. Richard & Levine (2025) propose observable microskills to tell the two apart. CAP’s silent dosing window is a college-level version of Track A behavior even when a psychologist is in the room. Licensed teams must **name which one they are doing** and bill only what occurred.

### CPSBC ketamine (professional guideline v1.4, 26 August 2026)

IV only in hospital or accredited non-hospital facility. Other routes possible with caution. Monitor dissociation and **post-dose sedation**, not vitals alone. Diversion is an explicit expectation. Off-label unknowns documented. Uninsured-service fee fairness; advertising standard; COI; sale-and-dispensing rules.

**Spravato / Janssen Journey** is the live REMS analog: enrolled prescriber and pharmacist, patient enrollment, supervised self-administration, post-dose monitoring. The drug does not go home as a casual take-home.

### CPHA 2024 (public health discussion)

Commercial investment outrunning evidence; Indigenous misappropriation risk; equity of who is in trials and clinics; public-health regulation balancing safety and access; harm-reduction education for the illegal market that already exists (storefronts, microdosing, underground guides). Corporate determinants of health (alcohol/tobacco/cannabis lessons) apply to psychedelic clinics.

### Brittain et al., *Journal of Bioethical Inquiry* (August 2025)

Scoping review of 51 articles. Literature clusters: standards of practice, equity, integrity, cultural appropriation, epistemic justice. Workshop gaps: **post-trial care**, no consensus on care models or practitioner competencies, translation of research ethics into ordinary prescribing.

### Touch empirics (2025–2026)

McHerron et al. 2025: flexible consent plus alliance can slide into boundary drift; **limits on expanding consent after dosing**. Neuroethics 2026 qualitative study: consent as process; name long-term psychological and social change and disappointment; define sexual vs therapeutic touch with body-map specificity, including atypical erogenous zones; train on nonverbal withdrawal cues.

### Ibogaine

hERG/QT prolongation, Torsades, deaths even under medical supervision; CYP2D6 variability; continuous ECG and crash-capable setting. Microdosing does **not** waive cardiac screening (Brunt 2026).

### Religious and sacramental law (not a clinic loophole)

- Federal peyote for Native American Church; state cultivation bans can still apply off-reservation.
- RFRA + *Gonzales v. UDV* (2006): sincere religious ayahuasca use; RFRA binds federal, not automatically state, law.
- Health Canada s.56 (from 2017): UDV and Santo Daime exemptions to import/use ayahuasca for **religious practice**—not a medical-practice authorization.
- A medical clinic cannot bootstrap RFRA by adding a chapel; a church exemption does not authorize medical claims.

### Ketamine-clinician three-role split

Mental-health professional (intake, prep, in-session psych support, integration, psych emergency); medical professional (physical safety); patient. One person may hold both professional roles only if actually qualified in both.

### Concepts 21–24 (third proposal)

21. **Name support versus psychotherapy and bill only what occurred.**
22. **Drug-class risk floor (ibogaine ≠ ketamine ≠ classic).** Match staff and equipment to the molecule, not to a generic “journey” protocol.
23. **Do not blur medical, sacramental, and adult-use frames.**
24. **Aftercare, fees, ads, and virtual limits as ethics.** Virtual prep/integration may help access; it does not replace in-person dosing accountability. Warn about compelled records before the patient speaks in a session that will be charted.

## Full proposed concept list (24)

These sit **on top of** existing license ethics (AMA, CMA, APA, CPA, college standards). They are for physicians, NPs, PAs, psychiatrists, and, where relevant, nurses, pharmacists, and psychotherapists on the team.

### 1. Indication honesty and least-misleading frame

Offer a psychedelic only for a documented clinical indication after assessing reasonably available alternatives. Name the evidence grade. If use is off-label, investigational, trial, or SAP/compassionate, say so. Do not present mystical or personality change as a medical benefit unless it is part of the disclosed, evidence-based indication. CANMAT still treats serotonergic psychedelics as experimental for MDD.

*Grounded in:* AMA 2.1.1; APA psychiatry position; CANMAT 2023; Alberta s.4; CPSA ketamine guidance.

### 2. Capacitous consent before ingestion, freeze on optional new permissions

Obtain consent only while the patient has decision-making capacity. Cover diagnosis, drug, dose range, time course, cardiovascular and psychiatric risks, interactions, possibility of no benefit, possible lasting change in values or relationships, presence of others, recording, touch policy, fees, and conflicts. After ingestion, the patient may always tighten restrictions. The clinician may not loosen restrictions or add optional interventions refused pre-dose, except true safety/emergency care. Do not expand touch consent after dosing.

*Grounded in:* AMA 2.1.1; CPA freedom of consent; HOPE V; Alberta capacity rule; McHerron 2025.

### 3. Three-tier touch plus body-map double consent

**Safety touch** (vitals, prevent fall, stop self-injury) needs no extra permission beyond the general safety plan. **Supportive touch** requires a written pre-dose agreement specifying where, why, who initiates, and stop signals, plus in-the-moment consent. **Sexual touch** (including kissing, cuddling-as-intimacy, genital/chest/mouth contact, atypical erogenous zones) is never permitted. In-session refusal always wins. Pre-dose refusal of supportive touch is not overridden because the intoxicated patient “asks.” After termination, default to the stricter of the health-profession sexual-boundary rule and a multi-year cooling-off (MAPS: never; CRPO/RHPA: five years plus ongoing power imbalance; Colorado facilitators: two years).

*Grounded in:* MAPS 5–6; JAMA consensus; APA psychiatry resource document; FSMB 2020; Neuroethics 2026 qualitative study.

### 4. Anti-capture: beliefs, money, testimonials, wills

During and immediately after the acute effect, do not solicit business, donations, investments, media, or sexual/romantic contact. Do not interpret the patient’s experience as proof of the clinician’s religion, politics, or product. No influence over wills or powers of attorney. Clinics disclose their philosophical setting in advance. Advertising uses outcome ranges, not guru narratives or testimonials obtained in a suggestible window.

*Grounded in:* HOPE VIII and X; APA 5; NASW 1.06; CRPO 1.8; CMA never exploit.

### 5. Medical screening, setting, rescue, no casual deprescribing

Complete medical and psychiatric assessment, medication review, and contraindication screen precede dosing. Someone competent to distinguish expected drug effects from emergency is present throughout. Characterize how long impairment lasts. Do not strip other psychotropics without a documented plan. Cardiac and autonomic monitoring scaled to the drug. Discharge includes after-effect counselling and a no-drive / no-high-stakes-decision window.

*Grounded in:* Alberta ss.3, 10–12, 16–17; FDA PDAC 2024; MAPS 1; CAP contraindications.

### 6. Track A: do not smuggle in unlicensed psychotherapy *(stronger in Track A)*

If talk therapy is not part of the service, say so. Acute-session speech is limited to safety, reassurance without interpretive depth, and following pre-agreed intentions. Do not run trauma processing, regression, or meaning-making the clinician is not contracted and licensed to provide. Offer a written list of independent integration resources. Brief medical follow-up (sleep, mood, suicidality, blood pressure, delayed distress) is required and is clinical monitoring, not PAT.

*Grounded in:* JAMA gatekeeping distinction; APA competence; CAP silent dosing window.

### 7. Track B: licensed therapy frame, two people, independent training *(stronger in Track B)*

Preparation, dosing support, and integration follow a named method and a therapy consent. Prefer two trained persons during dosing. Training is not solely by a product sponsor. Consented video is a legitimate abuse-deterrent, stored as a health record. Dual prescriber-therapist roles need extra supervision. Alberta psychologists: five years’ independent practice; no medical recommending.

*Grounded in:* Health Canada two-therapist notice; FDA PDAC; MAPS 4; APA 3.05; CAP 2025; RANZCP.

### 8. Fiduciary conflict vs conventional care and vs volume

Before recommending a psychedelic, disclose ownership, revenue share, speaker fees, equity, research funding, and dosing-volume incentives that could bias against SSRIs, trauma-focused therapy, ECT, or no treatment. Uninsured fees stated clearly and fairly.

*Grounded in:* Alberta s.4(c); ABA 1.7; CPSBC uninsured services; CPHA commercial determinants.

### 9. Confidentiality including groups, command, recordings, and compelled records

Session content, including spiritual material, is health information. Discuss mandatory reporting, duty to warn, and who else will be in the room before dosing. Recordings are not marketing assets. Group members get a confidentiality agreement. Military patients are told what command can learn. **Warn that WCB, insurers, employers, and courts may compel psychedelic notes.**

*Grounded in:* AMA 3.3.2; MAPS 2; CAP 2025 third-party warning.

### 10. Equity plus Indigenous eight Rs, not branding

Do not restrict access by race, gender, disability, or ability to pay beyond genuine safety criteria. Do not medicalize or obstruct lawful Indigenous or religious sacramental use. Do not claim ceremonial authority the clinician does not have. Do not patent or enclose traditional medicines. If the practice commercially depends on Indigenous knowledge, put benefit-sharing and FPIC in writing. Reverence includes source ecosystems.

*Grounded in:* Celidwen 2023; JAMA reciprocity; ANA 9–10; CNA values 4–5; CPHA.

### 11. Competence without mandatory personal use; change-of-scope when needed

Documented training: pharmacology, emergency, consent, boundaries, and (for Track B) the therapy method. Personal psychedelic experience is neither required nor a credential. If a clinician uses these drugs, it must be lawful and must not impair care. Clinicians do not dose while providing the service. Family physicians and others entering this work complete change-of-scope where the college requires it.

*Grounded in:* JAMA personal-experience consensus; CPSO ICRC; APA 2; CAP 5-year rule.

### 12. Impaired-patient protective action, limited to harm

Borrow ABA 1.14: keep treating the person as a decision-maker for preferences already expressed. Take protective action only to prevent reasonably certain death, serious bodily harm, or serious self-harm. Do not use “they are not themselves” as a license to pursue the clinician’s preferred narrative of healing.

*Grounded in:* ABA 1.14; FLSC impaired-client duties; AMA emergency-consent exception.

### 13. Continuity, delayed harm, non-abandonment

Psychedelic care is a course, not a session. Provide coverage through the acute and early integration window. Screen for delayed distress, mania, psychosis, suicidal thinking, and relational rupture. Terminate only with referral. Plan post-trial/post-course care before the first session.

*Grounded in:* MAPS 1; APA 10.09–10.10; Brittain 2025 post-trial-care gap.

### 14. Accountability that can leave the building

Written policies for consent, touch, abuse reporting, emergency, and quality review. Patients are told how to complain to the clinic **and** to the licensing college/board and, where relevant, FDA/Health Canada. Peer consultation for boundary dilemmas. Adverse events recorded in a form that can improve the field without privacy overreach.

*Grounded in:* Alberta ss.12–18; FDA advisers on external reporting; APA 1.

### 15. Clinical equipoise and no ballot-box medicine

Update beliefs as evidence accrues. Do not treat breakthrough-therapy labels, media, or state legalization as proof of efficacy. Until a product is approved for the indication, default is research or explicitly labelled off-label/SAP with corresponding consent. Health Canada SAC: established options first.

*Grounded in:* APA psychiatry position and resource document; CANMAT 2023; Helsinki 2024; SAC 2026.

### 16. Enhanced consent for unknowable and identity-level effects

Tell patients they may not be able to imagine the experience or its effect on values, politics, metaphysics, or religion; some will welcome that, some will not. Use testimony and educational materials, not hype. Preparation sessions extend consent but also prime set—name that tension. Consent remains possible when the patient understands they seek change from a bad status quo and can opt out of identity-level risk. Disclose possible disappointment if there is no response.

*Grounded in:* Smith & Sisti 2020; Jacobs 2023; Villiger 2024; APA psychiatry §3; CAP NOSC consent.

### 17. Most-responsible clinician in the dosing-day chain

A licensed clinician competent in the drug’s medical risks is responsible for the dosing day. Virtual check-ins plus unlicensed or lightly supervised staff for IV/IM or high-dose work fail college standards. RANZCP: the psychiatrist carries overall responsibility for selection, consent, monitoring, and follow-up. Nurses retain independent reporting duties. Three-role split (mental health / medical / patient) unless one person is truly dual-qualified.

*Grounded in:* CPSO ICRC; RANZCP; ANA provisions 3–4; CNA 2.1.5; ketamine clinician ethics; CPSBC monitoring.

### 18. No take-home psychedelic; pharmacist as independent stop

Do not dispense classic psychedelics or MDMA for unsupervised home use. Follow Spravato-like supervised administration where that analog fits. Pharmacists refuse prescriptions that are recreational, enhancement-seeking, or outside the prescriber’s scope, and run ordinary CDS reconciliation.

*Grounded in:* TGA AP information; Janssen Journey / Spravato; APhA/OCP.

### 19. Treatment not enhancement; harm-reduction talk without illegal assistance

Prescribe only for diagnosable conditions with functional impairment. Do not medicalize ordinary life stress or creativity/productivity quests. If a patient will use illicitly, discuss adulteration, NPS, setting, and emergency plans; do not procure, dose, or supervise illegal sessions; refer to lawful trials or adult-use regimes without making unapproved medical claims. Public-health education is a duty; illegal storefronts are not a clinic.

*Grounded in:* APA psychiatry §§5–6; AMA III; CNA harm-reduction theme; CPHA.

### 20. Strictest-license sexual boundary and special-population gates

Sexual/romantic contact with the patient is never therapeutic. Apply the strictest cooling-off rule of any license on the team. Extra gates: adolescents only with exceptional benefit and pediatric expertise; prisoners only with independent consent and no sentence-incentive coercion; end-of-life consent early, while capacitous; groups get extra confidentiality and no-pairing rules.

*Grounded in:* FSMB 2020; CRPO; Helsinki 27–28; palliative ethics; military JME 2024.

### 21. Name support versus psychotherapy and bill only what occurred

State in consent whether the dosing window is **psychological support** (safety, presence, non-directive) or **psychotherapy** (a named method with interpretive techniques). Use observable behaviors, not branding. Do not charge for PAT if the hour was monitoring. CAP: dosing intended silent/non-directive; integration is where meaning-making belongs.

*Grounded in:* Richard & Levine 2025; Goodwin debate; CAP 2025.

### 22. Drug-class risk floor (ibogaine ≠ ketamine ≠ classic)

**Ibogaine/noribogaine:** continuous ECG, QT management, CYP2D6 caution, setting that can treat Torsades; deaths have occurred under supervision; microdosing does not waive this. **Ketamine:** treat as sedation/dissociation with diversion control; IV only in accredited facilities where colleges require it. **Classic psychedelics:** psych-rescue, suggestibility, and delayed distress. Match staff, equipment, and exclusion criteria to the molecule, not to a generic “journey” protocol.

*Grounded in:* Brunt 2026; CPSBC ketamine; FDA PDAC cardiac data gap.

### 23. Do not blur medical, sacramental, and adult-use frames

Lawful NAC peyote, UDV/Santo Daime ayahuasca, Oregon/Colorado adult-use, and licensed medical care are different legal and ethical systems. A licensed clinician diagnosing and dispensing does not become a church; a church exemption does not authorize medical claims or off-label psychiatric treatment. Refer across frames without laundering one into another.

*Grounded in:* *Gonzales v. UDV*; Health Canada s.56; JAMA gatekeeping; CPHA intersections.

### 24. Aftercare, fees, ads, and virtual limits as ethics

Plan post-dose and post-trial/post-course care before the first session. State uninsured prices fairly; advertising must not outrun evidence. Virtual prep/integration may help access; it does not replace in-person dosing accountability. Warn about compelled records before the patient speaks in a session that will be charted.

*Grounded in:* Brittain 2025; CPSBC fees/ads; CAP WCB warning; SAC measurement-based care.

## Decision rule when capacity is reduced

| Patient state | What the clinician may do | What the clinician may not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before dosing, capacitous | Bind optional preferences (touch, music, visitors, metaphysics-neutrality, recording) | Hide experimental status, alternatives, or conflicts |
| During acute effect | Safety touch, emergency care, honor stop signals, follow the pre-dose plan | Add sexual, financial, or new optional-touch agreements; recruit for other products; impose a belief system |
| After the session, recovered | Revisit preferences, obtain new consent for a later session, offer or refer integration | Treat in-session utterances as durable legal or sexual consent |

## Assistant summaries as given in chat

### After pass 1

Licensed psychedelic practice should keep the ethical floor of medicine and law and add duties that ordinary prescribing does not cover. Two tracks, one floor. Fourteen concepts. Capacity rule: bind optional preferences before dosing; during the effect only safety plus the pre-dose plan; after recovery, new consent—never treat intoxicated speech as sexual or financial consent.

### After pass 2

The second pass showed licensed psychedelic care is a **team-and-regulator problem**. Psychiatry still says evidence is inadequate outside investigational studies. Nursing, pharmacy, social work, and CRPO each keep independent duties. FDA advisers treated unblinding, suggestibility, and sexual misconduct as benefit–risk. Australia already runs psychiatrist-only authorised prescribing with no anticipated take-home. Six new concepts (15–20), including most-responsible clinician, no take-home, treatment-not-enhancement, and strictest-license sexual boundary.

### After pass 3

Canadian colleges now specify day-to-day duties: experimental status (CANMAT), psychologists must not recommend the drug (CAP), ketamine-as-sedation (CPSBC), established options first (Health Canada SAC). Four new concepts (21–24): name support vs psychotherapy; drug-class risk floor; do not blur medical/sacramental/adult-use frames; aftercare/fees/ads/virtual limits/compelled records as ethics.
